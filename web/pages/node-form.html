Answer first. This file asks for one node of the design to change — or for a new one — and it is the whole of the request: it needs no connection and nothing installed. Fill in what you know, say why it is changing, save a copy and send it to the maintainer. They check it, build it in, and send you a preview of your change to try; when it gives what you expect, you approve it, and the next release carries it for everyone.

  1. What is asked. Every question the node answers, in the order it is read: what it is called and asks, said simply, the relation and where it comes from, the answer and its bounds, what it reads, and where the simple version breaks. Each question says why it is asked, and Show the example beside it shows the same question answered for one worked node. Leave what you cannot answer as it is.
  2. The method, your code, your cases. Write the relation once more as a method — a few lines in a small fixed language — paste the code you wrote and tested, and give at least three test cases your code answered and one it refuses. The page runs your method on your cases as you type, with the same checker the maintainer runs: a case that disagrees is shown before you send anything. Flight software that belongs to this node can be kept here with its test.
  3. Why it is changing. A node changes because a belief broke. If your changes move what the node computes — an input, the output, the model, the maths, the algorithm, how it is drawn — the section Why it is changing must say what was believed, what was tested, what we now know and what changes. Without it only your wording is applied. A worked example is in that section.
  4. What is not yours to change here. Where the node sits in the tree, its kind and its owner are the developers'. If an assistant helped with the relation itself — the equation, its steps, its derivation, the method, your code or your cases — say so: those are then never taken from the form. Known values go under known values, with their source.
