## Derivation

1. On a circle of radius $r$ at constant speed $v$, the acceleration toward the centre is $v^{2}/r$.
2. Gravity supplies it: $\mu / r^{2}$ per unit mass.
3. Setting them equal, $v^{2}/r = \mu/r^{2}$, gives $v = \sqrt{\mu/r}$.

## Assumptions

- Two-body point-mass gravity with $\mu = 3.986004418 \times 10^{14}\ \mathrm{m^{3}/s^{2}}$.
- A circular orbit.
- A radius inside the Earth is refused.

## Validity

For radii from 6,528 km to 6,828 km (150 km to 450 km altitude), while drag changes the speed by much less than a percent per orbit.
