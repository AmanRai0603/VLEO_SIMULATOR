## In one line

At 400 km a circular orbit needs 7.67 km/s — about 27,600 km/h — and higher orbits need less.

## Said simply

Throw a ball sideways and it falls in a curve. Throw it faster and it lands further away. Throw it fast enough and the ground curves away as fast as the ball falls: it never lands. That speed is the circular-orbit speed.

Further from the Earth, gravity is weaker, so it bends the path less and a slower speed is enough to stay on the circle.

## Where it breaks

Below about 300 km the air is thick enough to slow the spacecraft every orbit, so it must keep thrusting to hold this speed. An orbit that is not a circle has a speed that changes around it.

## Common misreading

"Faster means higher." To climb, a spacecraft first speeds up — and then, once on the higher circle, it moves more slowly than before. The {{eq E1}} relation is for the circle, not the climb.
