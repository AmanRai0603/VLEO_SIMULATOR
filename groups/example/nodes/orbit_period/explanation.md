## In one line

At 400 km one orbit takes 92.6 minutes — about 15.5 orbits a day.

## Said simply

The period is just the length of the circle divided by the speed. Going higher makes the circle longer and the speed lower, so both push the same way: higher orbits take longer.

## Where it breaks

The Earth turns underneath the orbit, so the spacecraft does not pass over the same place each orbit. And the flattening of the Earth makes the orbit's plane turn slowly; the period here is the time round the circle, not the time to pass over the same ground again.

## Common misreading

"A higher orbit is longer only because the circle is bigger." The spacecraft also moves more slowly up there — both effects add, which is why the period grows faster than the radius.
