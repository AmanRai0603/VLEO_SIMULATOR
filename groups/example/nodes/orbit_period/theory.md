## Derivation

1. One orbit is the circumference $2\pi r$, travelled at speed $v$: $T = 2\pi r / v$.
2. With $v = \sqrt{\mu / r}$, $T = 2\pi r \sqrt{r/\mu} = 2\pi\sqrt{r^{3}/\mu}$.

## Assumptions

- Two-body point-mass gravity, circular orbit.
- A radius inside the Earth is refused.

## Validity

For radii from 6,528 km to 7,378 km. Kepler's third law in this form holds for any circular orbit of the two-body problem.
