## In one line

The design is driven by the day's mean Kp, the mean of its eight three-hour slots: value 0 (1 would be its worst slot). Decided by Aman Rai on 2026-10-03.

## Said simply

Kp is graded every three hours, so each day has eight readings. The solar group publishes two of them for each scenario, the typical one and the worst one, and this row says which one the design is built on: the typical one, the day's mean, which is what the legacy run used and what a daily-averaged density model wants.

## Picture it

Think of designing a roof gutter for a stormy day. You could size it for the day's average rain or for its heaviest hour; both are true facts about the same day, but which one you build to is a decision, not a measurement. This row is that decision for the Sun's magnetic storms, and it is still open.

## Where it breaks

The mean is not what a vehicle meets in the worst three hours of a storm day: there the peak is higher, Kp 7.997 against 5.802 on the disturbed single day. The sheet names a third answer, sizing on the mean and closing against the peak, which would make this row a set rather than a switch; that is a later decision, not this one.

## Common misreading

That the legacy run has already settled it. Its header says the mean slot, so the study answered this for its own run, but the sheet records that as evidence rather than a recommendation and asks for the choice to be made, not defaulted.
