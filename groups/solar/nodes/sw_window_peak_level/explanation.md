## Said simply

The highest level the expected solar flux reaches during the mission, as opposed to its average. Over the declared five years the expectation opens near 105 and ends near 73: its average is 86.8, its peak 110.7. Thrust, propellant, intake area and lifetime are sized by the busiest sustained stretch, not the typical one.

## Picture it

Picture a long road trip that starts in the hills and ends on the plains. The average height of the road tells you little about whether the car can climb the highest pass, and the engine has to be sized for the pass. The mission's expected solar flux is the same: this row is the highest pass, not the average height.

## Where it breaks

It is the peak of the expectation, not of the sky: single days reach 343 while this row can never exceed 225.1. It says how high, not for how long. For future cycles it uses the midpoint of two past cycles 41 per cent apart, so if the next cycle runs like cycle 23 it is 17 per cent low.

## Common misreading

That it is a worst case. It is the highest the expected level goes, with no excursion on top; a level that will not be exceeded needs the uncertainty growth added.
