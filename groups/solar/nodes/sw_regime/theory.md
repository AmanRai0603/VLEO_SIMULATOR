## Equations

```
regime(Ap) = 1 if Ap <= 6, 2 if Ap <= 25, 3 otherwise
```

## Derivation

A design cannot respond to a continuous Ap. There is no distinct reaction to Ap 26 as against Ap 27, and a subsystem that tried to have one would be reacting to the measurement's noise. What a design can respond to is a regime: a small set of states, each with its own budget. The study did not choose the boundaries of those states by hand. It fitted a three-component mixture to the distribution of daily Ap and let the record decide where one population ends and the next begins. This node ports the classifier's DECISION, not its machinery, and the derivation below is why one line of thresholds is a faithful port of a fitted mixture.

1. Start from what a mixture model assumes: that the observed distribution of daily Ap is not one population but a sum of several, each Gaussian, each with its own share of the days, its own centre and its own width. For Ap the study used three — a quiet background, a disturbed population, and a broad storm tail. `p(Ap) = w1·N(Ap; m1, s1) + w2·N(Ap; m2, s2) + w3·N(Ap; m3, s3), w1 + w2 + w3 = 1`
2. Fitting chooses the nine numbers that make the observed record most likely. Which algorithm did that is not recorded here and does not matter to this row: prf_cluster.m is not in this repository, only its output, and what is ported is where the fit ended up, not how it got there.
3. Once fitted, each day gets a posterior share of each component — how much of that day the component accounts for. These are the three numbers daily_regime.csv publishes beside every date. `g_i(Ap) = w_i·N(Ap; m_i, s_i) / SUM_j w_j·N(Ap; m_j, s_j)`
4. The label is whichever component claims the largest share. That is the whole classifier: everything above exists to produce this argmax, and the study published the argmax as its 'regime' column. `label(Ap) = argmax_i g_i(Ap)`
5. Now the step that turns a fitted mixture into thresholds. Two components swap the lead exactly where their weighted densities cross, and in one dimension that condition is a QUADRATIC in Ap — take logs of the ratio and the squares do not cancel unless the two widths are equal. So each pair of components crosses at up to two values of Ap, not one. `log[ w_i·N(Ap; m_i, s_i) / w_j·N(Ap; m_j, s_j) ] = 0 is quadratic in Ap`
6. Where the three components have separated widths, the useful root of each quadratic lands between the two centres, and the argmax becomes an ordered partition of the Ap axis. Read off the published labels, both boundaries sit on integers: the quiet-to-active swap falls between 6 and 7, and the active-to-storm swap between 25 and 26. Nothing of the mixture survives except those two numbers. `regime(Ap) = 1 for Ap <= 6, 2 for 7 <= Ap <= 25, 3 for Ap >= 26`
7. The other root is not harmless, and it is measurable in the published file. A broad component's density falls off more slowly than a narrow one's, so far enough down the LOW tail the storm component overtakes quiet again. In daily_regime.csv the mean storm posterior climbs as Ap falls — 0.199 at Ap 3, 0.332 at Ap 2, 0.726 at Ap 1, 0.992 at Ap 0 — crossing the quiet posterior between Ap 2 and Ap 1. That second crossing is the whole of the artefact: 140 days at Ap 1 and 31 at Ap 0, all labelled storm, 171 in total. `g_storm > g_quiet for Ap <= 1 as well as for Ap >= 26`
8. This row keeps the first root of each pair and cuts the second. A design told 'storm' on a day with Ap 1 is being actively misled, and the departure is one rule rather than a list of dates. On the other 10128 days the three-band relation reproduces the published label exactly — the disagreement count is zero, not small.

## Assumptions

- It departs from the published table on 171 of 10299 days, deliberately. Fails when: parity with the study is what is wanted. Every one of the 171 is a day the mixture labelled 'storm' with an Ap of 0 or 1 and a Kp_max of 0 or 1, in 2006 to 2011. This row calls them quiet. On the other 10128 days it reproduces the published label exactly — the disagreement count against the corrected labels is zero, not small. Anyone reconciling against daily_regime.csv will find exactly these days and no others, and the sign of the disagreement is always the same: the table says storm where this row says quiet, never the reverse
- The only Ap this tree publishes is a design storm, so the quiet band is unreachable today. Fails when: somebody expects to see all three answers in a run. The input is sw_storm_return_level, whose declared range is 20 to 230, so in any run this node can only return active or storm — and at the declared five-year mission it returns 3. The relation carries all three branches because the classifier has three, and the day an ordinary Ap exists in the tree this row already handles it. That no such row exists is a fact about the tree and is worth noticing rather than working around
- Three bands on one variable, which is all the study's mixture turned out to be. Fails when: a regime is expected to depend on anything but today's Ap. It does not: no history, no rate of change, no F10.7. A day at Ap 26 on the way up and a day at Ap 26 on the way down are the same regime here, and a storm's recovery day is labelled by its level rather than by what it is recovering from. That is the study's model and this row is a port of it, not an improvement on it
- The boundaries are exact integers and the published confidences are discarded. Fails when: a caller wants to know how sure the label is. daily_regime.csv carries a posterior per day — mean 0.550 for quiet, 0.580 for active, 0.705 for storm, with 21 per cent of quiet days below 0.5 — and this row publishes one number with no confidence beside it. The bands being exact makes the LABEL certain given the Ap; it does not make the label right. A day at Ap 26 is one unit from being called active and the study was only 55 to 70 per cent sure of its labels on average

## Validity

From 1 to 3 One. Below: 1 is quiet and is the lowest regime the mixture defines. Below it there is no label. Above: 3 is storm and is the highest. A fourth regime would be a different classifier than the one this row ports.

The answer is an index, not a severity: 3 is not three times 1, and the distance from 1 to 2 is nineteen units of Ap while the distance from 2 to 3 is one. It says which population today belongs to and nothing about how far into that population it is — a day at Ap 26 and a day at Ap 200 are both 3.

It also carries no confidence, although the classifier it ports published one: the bands being exact makes the label certain GIVEN the Ap, which is a different claim from the label being right. On the record the study was 55 to 70 per cent sure of its own labels on average, and 21 per cent of its quiet days scored below 0.5.
