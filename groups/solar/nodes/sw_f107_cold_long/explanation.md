## Said simply

The low level of the Sun's flux the design must still work at, held over the mission window: the expected centre with a margin taken off, about 69.6 solar flux units. A cold Sun means thin air and less drag, which is the case with the least aerodynamic control, the slowest drag-based phasing, the longest end-of-life de-orbit and the least power into the array.

## Picture it

Think of a sailing boat that steers with the wind. The danger is not only a gale but a calm, when the sails have nothing to push on. A cold Sun is the calm: thin air, little drag and the least aerodynamic control. This row is the sustained calm the design must still work in, about 69.6 solar flux units.

## Where it breaks

The margin of 1.28 spreads is really a 90th-percentile edge while the run is called 95 per cent. The spread is treated as symmetric though forecast errors are skewed, which makes this edge a little too cold, and one spread is used across a whole window even though it changes over the cycle.

## Common misreading

The cold side is easy to read as the harmless one. Thin air is where drag-based control is weakest and de-orbit is slowest; a tool that publishes only the hot edge has told half the problem.
