## Said simply

How far off the pattern-based forecast of the next 27-day stretch of geomagnetic disturbance has typically been, measured over 361 past forecasts that each used only earlier data. It is about 3.59 on the daily index, and it sets the width of the sustained design bands on both the disturbed and the quiet side.

## Picture it

Imagine someone guessing next month's average from the pattern of the months before, and you keep score of every miss. They do it 361 times, each time using only what was known before the guess. The typical size of the miss is this row: about 3.59 on the daily Ap index.

## Where it breaks

One spread is used for the whole cycle, though geomagnetic activity is burstier in the declining phase, so a window there gets a band too narrow. The index cannot go below zero and its errors are lopsided, so one symmetric spread fits the middle better than the far tail. Nine missing months of 2017 were filled in; counted as real, they would make the spread look narrower.

## Common misreading

It is easy to take this as how far Ap swings from one day to the next. It is how far the forecast of a whole 27-day stretch's average has missed; how far a single day departs from its own stretch is a separate number, measured in sw_ap_daily_band_spread.
