## Said simply

How far above its own 81-day average the Sun's flux must climb for a day to count as a spike: 1.3049 times that average. Measuring against the day's own baseline matters because a level that is ordinary at solar maximum is extraordinary at minimum, and the threshold comes from the record's own scatter rather than a round number.

## Where it breaks

It tests a single day and says nothing about how long the flux stays high; that needs the event-duration row beside it. The average is centred, so it needs 40 days of the future: it describes a record and cannot be a live trigger. Nine months of 2017 are missing, so any spikes then are not counted.

## Common misreading

That 'two and a half standard deviations' carries the usual bell-curve odds. The ratio is long-tailed upward: the largest in the record, 2.11, is nine standard deviations above the mean.
