## Said simply

Everything the solar-weather subsystem concludes, carried up to the system level as one set: five design scenarios (expected, sustained hot, sustained quiet, hottest day, quietest day), each with a daily solar-flux level, its 81-day mean, and a geomagnetic disturbance level. It copies them unchanged, so a system reader never has to open the subsystem.

## Picture it

Think of a surveyor handing a client one summary sheet instead of the field notebooks. The sheet lists the same figures the notebooks reached, copied rather than reworked, so the client can act without opening the notebooks. The catch is the same too: every caveat in the notebooks stays behind.

## Where it breaks

Every value inherits the limits of the row beneath it and a system reader sees none of them: the sustained scenarios are 90th-percentile bands though labelled 95 per cent, and the day scenarios are nearer one-in-a-hundred days. One refused member refuses the whole set. The sheet also disagrees with itself on whether the geomagnetic storm-scale columns cross.

## Common misreading

Thinking the 81-day mean column is a calculation. For the day scenarios it is simply the mean level of the sustained scenario that day rides on; no values are combined.
