## Said simply

Kp is a scale from 0 to 9 of how disturbed Earth's magnetic field is. These are the ten Kp values the five driver scenarios carry: the typical and the worst three-hour stretch of each day. Converting the day's average straight to Kp reads quieter than the day's worst stretch, and a drag design is sized by exactly those days.

## Picture it

Think of a day's rain. The daily total, spread evenly over the hours, can come from steady drizzle or from one cloudburst, and a gutter only cares about the cloudburst. Ap is like the evenly spread figure; the two Kp slots are the typical hour and the heaviest hour. This row gives both, for each of the five scenarios.

## Where it breaks

The corrections are medians, so half the days at any activity level have a worse peak than the one published here; they know the day's average, not whether it was one long storm or quiet with one severe spike. The tables are held at their ends rather than extended, so a scenario pushed past them would reach that limit without warning.

## Common misreading

That converting the day's average activity onto the published Kp scale gives the Kp that matters. It lands above the typical three-hour slot and well below the day's peak, so a design sized on it alone is sized on a quieter sky than the record shows.
