## Said simply

How far, typically, the tool's pattern for solar flux misses the level of the next 27-day solar rotation: about 13.4544 solar flux units, measured over 361 rotations using only what was known before each one. It is the margin a design carries around its central expectation, which without it is a line with no width.

## Picture it

Imagine guessing each month's electricity bill from the usual bill for that time of year, nudged by how the last two months went. You write each guess down before the bill arrives, then compare. The typical gap between guess and bill is this row: how wide the line really is.

## Where it breaks

One number is pooled over the whole solar cycle, but the spread is not flat: near solar maximum this band is too narrow and near minimum too wide. A nine-month gap in 2017 was filled with smooth invented values, which makes the spread a little narrower than the record can support.

## Common misreading

That it measures what a forecaster would get wrong. There is no forecast in it, only the cycle pattern and the memory of the last two rotations; a real 27-day outlook does better.
