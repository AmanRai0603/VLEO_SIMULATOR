## Said simply

How closely one solar cycle repeats the shape of the one before, measured by comparing the last two complete cycles laid over each other by how far through the cycle each point is. The shapes match fairly well, at 0.77 on a scale where one is identical, but the sizes do not: the second cycle peaked at about three-quarters of the first.

## Picture it

Lay two children's growth charts over each other, stretched to the same age range. The curves can rise and level off in just the same way while one child ends up much taller. That is these two cycles: the shapes match at 0.77, the sizes do not.

## Where it breaks

There are only two complete cycles in the record, so this is one observation, not an estimate with a known uncertainty. The measure ignores size entirely, so it can call two cycles alike when one is far stronger, and nine months of missing data forced two of the twenty comparison slots out.

## Common misreading

A high match in shape is easy to read as meaning the cycles repeat. The measure cannot see size, and the two cycles differ by about a third of the mean; a mission sized on their average is sized for neither.
