## Equations

```
F107_req = 260 sfu, above the record's largest 27-day mean of 252.67
```

## Derivation

A requirement is not a prediction and not a capability: it is a commitment, and its purpose is to be checkable against something measured. This row commits the design to surviving a solar flux, and the checking partner is l3_solar_ach_01, which reports what the record actually presents. The pair is the closure, and the whole reason a requirement lives on its own row — rather than being inferred from the design value — is that a commitment which silently tracked the design could never be violated and would therefore never be a requirement.

1. Start from what a requirement row must be able to do: be violated. If the committed number were computed from the design value it would track every change to the design and would always pass, which makes the closure a tautology. So the value is DECLARED — a person's number with their name against it — and the sense says which side of it is safe. `F107 <= F107_req (sense: <=)`
2. The number started from the capability the design chain produced WHEN IT WAS WRITTEN, which was sw_f107_design — a one-sided 95th-percentile PERSISTENCE growth on the central expectation. That row is no longer the achieved side of this closure. sw_f107_design_long is, and it answers a different question: how far the pattern has historically been from the rotation level it predicted, which is the width of the window rather than the drift from today. `was F107_design = 200.14 sfu · now F107_long = 104.07 sfu`
3. THE NUMBER IS NOW ANCHORED IN THE RECORD RATHER THAN ABOVE THE CHAIN, and that is the substance of this revision. It was 250 — the next round figure above the 228.14 the chain achieved when the row was written, 9.6 per cent of headroom. Two later changes moved the achieved side to 104.07, so the same 250 became 140 per cent of headroom: a ceiling the environment could not approach, which is a closure that cannot fail, which is the one thing a requirement must be able to do. The number was tracking the design and not the sky. The anchor is the hottest sustained sky the record holds at the timescale this requirement is about. The requirement says SUSTAINED, and the achieved side is a band on rotation-mean forecasts, so the matching measure is the 27-day mean: the largest in 28.2 years is 252.67 sfu, centred on 2024-08-13. Round up to the next ten, the same rule l3_solar_req_02 uses on the record's largest single day, and the commitment is 260 sfu — the design operates through any rotation the record has ever shown. `260 / 252.67 = 1.029 against the record's hottest rotation · 260 / 104.07 = 2.498 against this window`
4. The number does not move with the epoch and is not meant to. The record runs 64 to 343 sfu across a cycle, so a mission starting in 2030 near minimum would find 260 enormously conservative — but the requirement is the vehicle's capability, and the sky's variation belongs on the achieved side of the closure where it can be seen.
5. This is the first `required` row written anywhere in this repository, so its shape is the shape sixteen other subsystems will copy. Two things about that shape are worth stating: the seed's kind = "required" means computed-with-a-sense, and a committed number like this one is kind = "declared" with a top-level sense.

## Assumptions

- 260 is the record's hottest sustained rotation rounded up, and the SATELLITE-ERA record is not the sun's history. Fails when: the record is read as the sun's history. 260 is anchored at 252.67 sfu, the largest 27-day mean in 28.2 years of SATELLITE-ERA record, centred 2024-08-13. Cycle 19 in the late 1950s ran higher than anything in that record, so a design meant to operate through a cycle-19 maximum needs a larger number and the rounding to 260 buys seven sfu against it. The anchor is at least checkable, which its predecessor was not: 250 was the next round number above whatever the chain happened to give, so it moved with the design rather than with the sky and its headroom drifted from 9.6 per cent to 140 without anything being wrong.
- It is a ceiling on the DRIVER and says nothing about what the driver does to the vehicle. Fails when: the requirement is read as a survivability statement. F10.7 is an index of solar radio flux; what a spacecraft actually feels is the density that flux produces at its altitude, through a model this subsystem does not own. A design that meets F10.7 <= 250 and is sized on a density model with the wrong drag coefficient has met this requirement and will still deorbit early. The closure this row takes part in is on the environment, and the environment is only the first half.
- One number for the whole mission, with no epoch and no phase in it. Fails when: the mission slips. The record runs 64 to 343 sfu across a cycle, so the flux a mission sees depends on where in the cycle it flies; the declared epoch of 2027-01-01 sits at phase 0.619, past maximum on the declining side. A mission starting in 2030 would sit near minimum and 250 would be enormously conservative. The requirement does not move with the epoch and is not meant to — it is the vehicle's capability, and the sky's variation belongs on the achieved side.

## Validity

From 60 to 400 One. Below: the same floor as env_f107: below 60 sfu has never been observed, so a requirement there could never be met and is not a requirement. Above: the same ceiling as env_f107: above 400 sfu every consumer of F10.7 is extrapolating, and a requirement written past the range its consumers support is not checkable.

It is a ceiling on the DRIVER and says nothing about what the driver does to the vehicle. F10.7 is an index of solar radio flux; what a spacecraft feels is the density that flux produces at its altitude, through a model this subsystem does not own. A design that meets F10.7 <= 260 and is sized with the wrong drag coefficient has met this requirement and will still deorbit early. The margin now HAS a derivation, which it did not when this row was written: 260 sfu is the record's largest 27-day mean of 252.67, centred 2024-08-13, rounded up to the next ten. Against this window's achieved 104.07 that is 150 per cent of headroom, and the headroom is no longer the point — the number says the design operates through any rotation the record has shown, and a reader can check it against the record rather than against whatever the chain happened to give.
