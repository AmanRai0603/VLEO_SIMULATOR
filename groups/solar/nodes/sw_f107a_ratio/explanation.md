## Said simply

How far a single day's solar flux typically strays from its own 81-day average, as a fraction so it applies at any activity level: about twelve per cent. It is the width that goes with the smoothed level; a density model driven only by the smoothed flux designs to the average day.

## Picture it

Think of a town's daily temperature against the usual temperature for that time of year. The usual figure tells you which coat to pack; the day-to-day wobble tells you whether you will still be cold on the day. This row is the Sun's wobble, written as a fraction of its own average so it means the same in a quiet year and a busy one.

## Where it breaks

The spread is lopsided: a day can sit far above its average, up to 2.11 times it, but never below zero, so doubling the spread does not give a 95 per cent band; the measured percentiles, 1.2188 and 1.3711, are the numbers to use. It is pooled over the whole cycle, though days stray more near the peak.

## Common misreading

It is tempting to build a symmetric 95 per cent band from two of these spreads. The distribution is bounded below and long-tailed above, so two spreads do not mean 95 per cent here.
