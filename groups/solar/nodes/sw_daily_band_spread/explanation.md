## Said simply

How far a single day of the Sun's flux can rise above its own 27-day average, at the one-in-twenty level and at the level that stretch sits at. At the disturbed design level it is about 20.07 solar flux units. It matters because an array is sized on the sustained level but a thermal case and a drag transient are sized on the day.

## Picture it

A 27-day average is like a monthly average temperature: it hides the hot afternoons. Take each day away from its own stretch's average and see how far the warmest day in twenty sits above it. Do that separately for quiet and busy stretches, because a quiet Sun stays close to its average and a busy one swings far.

## Where it breaks

Outside the measured range of 70 to 210 solar flux units the answer is held at the end value, so a mission at cycle peak above 210 gets a band narrower than the record supports. The top of the table rests on far fewer days than the bottom, and this is past scatter, not a forecast error.

## Common misreading

It can look like the old single number was a safe margin. It was measured on stretches averaging 136.63 and applied at other levels; at the design level it was 71 per cent too high, and on the quiet side it drove a scenario below the floor.
