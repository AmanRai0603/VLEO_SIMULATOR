## Said simply

How far a single day of geomagnetic disturbance can fall below its own 27-day average, at the one-in-twenty level and at the level that stretch sits at. It is measured from the daily record and read at the quiet design level, where it comes to about 12.56. It feeds the quietest single-day scenario, which sits closest to the zero floor.

## Picture it

Think of daily temperatures in a month: some days dip below the month's average. How far the coldest one-in-twenty day dips depends on what kind of month it is, so you look the dip up for that kind of month rather than using one number for all. This row does the same for the geomagnetic index, at the level the 27-day stretch sits at.

## Where it breaks

It is past scatter in days that already happened, not a forecast error. The drop is read from a table measured at eight levels and interpolated between them; the interpolation and the later subtraction do not know the index floors at zero, and the 27-day window is a conventional round number rather than a measured period.

## Common misreading

It is easy to treat this as the upward swing turned around. The upward swing is about twice the drop at the bottom of the table and three times at the top, so mirroring one onto the other is wrong by that factor.
