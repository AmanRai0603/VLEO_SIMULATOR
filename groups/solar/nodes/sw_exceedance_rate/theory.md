## Equations

```
R_exc(Ap_design) = days with Ap >= Ap_design, per year of record
```

## Derivation

The requirement built on the G3 capability is violated: the record expects Ap 158.4 over the mission and the design is built for 132. A violated requirement is useless information on its own — what a programme has to decide is whether the violation is tolerable, and that decision needs three numbers rather than one: how often, how long, and when. This row is the first of the three. It converts 'the bound is exceeded' into 'the bound is exceeded on this many days a year', which is the form a decision can actually be taken on.

1. The measurement is a count and a division with nothing fitted in it: the days whose daily Ap reaches the design bound, over the years of record actually present. `R_exc(Ap_design) = #{ d : Ap(d) >= Ap_design } / 28.197 yr`
2. The denominator is the days present, not the calendar span. That matters because 273 days are absent: dividing by the span would dilute the rate by the length of the gap, as though nothing could have happened in it. Dividing by the days observed keeps the rate honest about what was measured and silent about what was not.
3. Counted at the three levels sw_ap_design can return, the rate falls steeply: 131 days above Ap 48, 35 above Ap 80, 8 above Ap 132. `G1: 4.646 /yr G2: 1.241 /yr G3: 0.284 /yr`
4. Which answers the design question at the declared level. Multiply by the mission and the violation has a size: at G3 the vehicle spends 1.42 days of a five-year mission outside what it was built for, at G2 6.21 days, at G1 23.2. `0.284 /yr * 5 yr = 1.42 d`
5. Notice what the three numbers rule out. A design that must NEVER be exceeded cannot be built at any level the G scale offers below G4, so the decision available is not whether to have exceedances but how many days of them to accept.
6. The relation is three anchors and a straight line, and the line is wrong. Exceedance rate against threshold falls close to exponentially — 4.65 to 1.24 to 0.284 is a factor of 3.7 then 4.4 — so linear interpolation overstates the rate badly between anchors. Nothing in the tree can reach between them, because the G scale is integer-valued and the producer's range is 1 to 3; the assumption is written so that the day something can, it is not believed.
7. One bias in the count has a known direction. The 273 absent days sit at cycle 24's phase 0.74 to 0.80, INSIDE the band where exceedances concentrate rather than in the quiet part of the cycle. So whatever happened there is missing from the numerator, and the true rate is more likely above this figure than below it.

## Assumptions

- The design bound IS exceeded, and the rate is the whole argument for accepting it. Fails when: the bound is read as a limit the sky respects. At G3, Ap 132, the record holds 8 days above it in 28.197 years — 0.284 a year, one day in 1287. Over the declared five-year mission that is 1.42 days. At G2, Ap 80, it is 35 days, 1.24 a year, 6.21 days per mission; at G1, Ap 48, it is 131 days, 4.65 a year, 23.2 days per mission. A design that must never be exceeded cannot be built at any of these levels, and the decision is about how many days of exceedance are tolerable rather than whether there are any
- Three points and a straight line between them, on a quantity that is anything but straight. Fails when: the answer is read anywhere except at 48, 80 or 132. Those are the only three values sw_ap_design can return, because the G scale is defined on integers and the producer's range is 1 to 3. The relation interpolates between them because Table1 must do something, and the interpolation is wrong: exceedance rate against threshold is close to exponential — 4.65, 1.24, 0.284 falls by a factor of 3.7 then 4.4 — so a linear reading between anchors overstates the rate badly in the middle. Nothing in the tree can reach those points, and this assumption exists so that the day something can, it is not believed
- It is a rate over a record, not a forecast, and the record is short where it matters. Fails when: the mission is the one that meets the rare event. 8 days above G3 in 28 years is a small sample: remove the single worst storm and the count is 7. The rate has a counting uncertainty of roughly its own square root, so 0.284 a year is 0.284 give or take 0.10 — a third of itself. Sizing on 1.42 days per mission when the honest range is roughly 0.9 to 2.0 is the correct use of it; treating 1.42 as a number with two decimal places of meaning is not
- 273 absent days, and they are not spread evenly over the risk. Fails when: the count is read as complete. observed_daily.csv is missing 2017-01-01 to 2017-09-30. That window sits on cycle 24's decline at phase 0.74 to 0.80, which is inside the band where exceedances concentrate — sw_exceedance_phase puts their median at 0.603 and their spread at 0.29 to 0.74. So the gap is in the risky part of the cycle, not the quiet part, and the true rate is more likely above this figure than below it. The denominator used is 28.197 years, the days actually present, so the gap does not dilute the rate; what it does is remove whatever happened in it

## Validity

From 0.1 to 6 One. Below: the lowest rate this relation can return is 0.284, at the G3 bound of Ap 132. A bound at 0.1 sits under it and catches a table read at the wrong end; it is NOT a claim that no design level is exceeded less often, because a higher bound than G3 is outside the declared G range. Above: the highest is 4.65, at the G1 bound of Ap 48. A bound at 6 sits just above it. A rate above 6 days a year would mean the design level had fallen to around Ap 40, which is below anything the G scale defines as a storm and below what sw_ap_design can return.

It is a rate over a record, not a forecast, and the record is short exactly where it matters. Eight days above the G3 bound in 28.2 years is a sample small enough that removing the single worst storm leaves seven; counting uncertainty is roughly the square root of the count, so 0.284 a year is 0.284 give or take about 0.10 — a third of itself. Sizing on 1.42 days per mission when the honest range is roughly 0.9 to 2.0 days is the correct use of the number. Treating 1.42 as having two decimal places of meaning is not.
