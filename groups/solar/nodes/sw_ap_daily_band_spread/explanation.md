## Said simply

How far a single day of geomagnetic disturbance can rise above its own 27-day average, at the one-in-twenty level and at the level that stretch sits at. At the disturbed design level it is about 63.85, more than four times the old single-number value of 15.00, and it roughly doubled the single-day design level.

## Picture it

A stormy month is not a calm month scaled up; it is a calm month with a few gales in it. So how high the windiest day climbs above its month's average depends heavily on which kind of month you are in. This row reads that climb at the level the disturbed stretch sits at, instead of borrowing it from quiet months.

## Where it breaks

The design level sits just above the highest level the table reaches, so the answer is held at the top value rather than extended; extending the steep top of the table would give about 72, so the published value may be low. The top of the table rests on only 300 days, and moves if one storm period is added or removed.

## Common misreading

The old single number looked like a margin but was an error: it was measured on stretches averaging 11.64 and applied at 26.70. A disturbed stretch is not a quiet stretch scaled up; it contains storms.
