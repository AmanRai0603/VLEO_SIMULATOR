## Said simply

The Sun's flux reading today, the starting point the forecast begins from. It is a round default of 150 solar flux units, not a reading taken on a date, and it matters very little: moving it across its whole allowed range leaves the expected central flux unchanged to six figures, because its weight fades within about a solar rotation.

## Picture it

Think of a weather forecast that starts from today's temperature. For tomorrow, today matters a lot; for the same date five years away, it tells you nothing. This row is today's reading for the Sun's flux, a round 150, and by the lead times this tool asks about its pull has faded away entirely.

## Where it breaks

It would start to matter at leads of a few days, where today's flux counts and a round 150 would be doing real work badly; this tree's declared leads start well past that. It is a daily value, not an 81-day average, and must not be read as one.

## Common misreading

It is easy to take this for the flux the design is sized to. It is only the forecast's starting anchor; the design flux is computed and published elsewhere in this group.
