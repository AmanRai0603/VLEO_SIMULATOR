## Said simply

How far a single day of the Sun's flux can fall below its own 27-day average, at the one-in-twenty level and at the level that stretch sits at. At the quiet design level it is about 4.39 solar flux units, seven times less than the old single-number value, and that correction lets the quietest single-day scenario publish a real value instead of refusing.

## Picture it

Think of water lapping in a shallow pond with a hard bottom. The waves can rise as high as they like, but they cannot dip far, because there is little room below. A quiet Sun sits near a floor in the same way, so at the quiet design level a one-in-twenty day falls only about 4.39 below its 27-day average.

## Where it breaks

The quiet design level sits just below the lowest level the table was measured at, so the answer is held at the end value; a colder window gets the same number. It is past scatter in days already observed, not a forecast error, and the 27-day window is a conventional round number, not a measured rotation period.

## Common misreading

Falls below the average are not rises above it turned around. The Sun has a floor and no ceiling, so the two differ by six to fifteen per cent depending on level. And the quiet side matters too: it sets the low-drag case.
