The number of days after which the Sun's flux best repeats itself once the eleven-year cycle is removed: 26 days.

## Said simply

The number of days after which the Sun's flux best repeats itself once the eleven-year cycle is removed: 26 days. The Sun rotates, so an active region facing Earth today comes back round about a month later; this is why a solar outlook is 27 days long, and the shortest time a design can expect a region to return.

## Picture it

Picture a merry-go-round with one brightly painted horse: stand still and it passes you again once every turn. A bright active region on the Sun does the same, so today's flux echoes about a month later. Unlike a merry-go-round, the Sun's equator turns faster than its mid-latitudes, so the echo is smeared over about a week.

## Where it breaks

The repeat is broad, not sharp: it stays strong from about 23 to 29 days, because the Sun's equator and mid-latitudes turn at different speeds and active regions grow and fade within a rotation. It is a tendency over a week-wide window, not a clock a design can time itself to.

## Common misreading

That 26 days is the rotation period. The period is 27 days, shown by later repeats at 54 and 81 days; the first peak reads one day short because it sits on a falling background.
