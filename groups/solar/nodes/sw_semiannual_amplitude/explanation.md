## Said simply

How big the twice-yearly swing in geomagnetic activity is: about 1.278 units of the daily index around a mean near 10.5, roughly 12 per cent. Activity runs higher near the equinoxes than the solstices; the point is the size, which shifts the average a little and predicts almost nothing about a given day.

## Picture it

Think of a busy road whose traffic is slightly heavier in spring and autumn. Any one day's count is ruled by accidents, events and weather, which swamp that seasonal drift; only by averaging months of counts do you see it at all. The twice-yearly swing in geomagnetic activity is that kind of drift.

## Where it breaks

The swing explains only 0.63 per cent of day-to-day variation, so it belongs in monthly or yearly budgets, not as a daily correction. It is a fixed amount added and subtracted, which understates the effect in active periods and overstates it in quiet ones, and one term cannot show that the autumn peak is higher than the spring one.

## Common misreading

That the seasonal swing helps predict a given day. Storm-to-storm variation exceeds the season by more than an order of magnitude; the swing is a shift in the average, visible only in aggregate.
