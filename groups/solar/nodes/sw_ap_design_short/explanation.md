## Said simply

The daily level of geomagnetic disturbance the design must survive on a single bad day inside the mission window: the sustained disturbed level plus a disturbed day's own rise above it. It is about 90.5, a strong storm day, and it is what drag transients and single-orbit attitude cases are sized on.

## Picture it

Think of a river in a wet season. Its level for weeks is already raised, but one stormy day can push it far higher than that. The sustained Ap, about 26.7, is the wet-season level; a disturbed day adds its own rise of about 63.85 on top, and that rise is most of the answer.

## Where it breaks

Most of the answer is the daily rise, which is held at the top of its measured table and may be low. The value now exceeds the commitment to survive a single day at 80, so the closure against that requirement fails; whether the commitment or the window is wrong is a decision, not arithmetic.

## Common misreading

The earlier 41.7 looked like a sound single-day level. It used a daily swing measured on much quieter stretches, and the record supports more than twice that for this window's own hot case.
