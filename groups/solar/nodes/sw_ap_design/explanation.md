## In one line

The daily level of geomagnetic disturbance the vehicle is built to survive, taken from the storm grade the design has chosen.

## Said simply

The daily level of geomagnetic disturbance the vehicle is built to survive, taken from the storm grade the design has chosen. At the default grade G3 it is 132, the top of that grade, and it sits below the 158.4 the record expects to see once over a five-year mission; that gap is the finding.

## Picture it

Think of a building designed to a storm category: you pick the category, and the design wind is the top of that category, not a typical storm in it. Here the design picks a storm grade, and the answer is the daily level at the top of that grade. Whether the sky will stay inside the grade is a separate question, answered elsewhere.

## Where it breaks

It is a bound, not a typical storm day: real G3 days in the record run from 15 to 96 with a median of 51. It knows only the size of a storm, not how long it lasts, so a one-day storm and the first day of a three-day storm look the same here, although a sustained storm heats the atmosphere far more.

## Common misreading

It is easy to read this and the once-per-mission storm level as competing answers. One is what the vehicle is built for and the other what the sky will present; the gap is closed by operations, not structure.
