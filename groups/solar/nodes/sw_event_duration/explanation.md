## Said simply

Once the Sun's flux jumps past the level this tool counts as a spike, how many days in a row it tends to stay there: on average about 3.3 days. A single raised day and a week of them are different loads on a drag budget and a power budget, so the length of a burst matters more than the crossing.

## Picture it

Think of hot spells in summer. One hot day and a week-long heatwave both begin with the same crossing of the same temperature, but they are very different loads on a household. This row counts how long each spell above the flux spike threshold lasted: 61 spells, about 3.3 days on average.

## Where it breaks

The average hides the shape: of 61 bursts, 24 lasted one day, the middle one lasted 3, and a few long ones of up to 10 days pull the average up. Any quiet day ends a burst, which is a choice with no physics behind it, and bursts inside the nine missing months of 2017 are lost or cut short.

## Common misreading

It is easy to size on 3.3 days as a typical burst. The most common burst is one day and the ones a design is exposed to run up to 10; 3.3 is neither the common case nor the bad one.
