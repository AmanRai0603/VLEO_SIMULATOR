## In one line

The storm level the design is built to survive, on the NOAA scale of geomagnetic storms from G1, minor, to G5, extreme.

## Said simply

The storm level the design is built to survive, on the NOAA scale of geomagnetic storms from G1, minor, to G5, extreme. It is set to G3, strong. It is a switch: the design's storm activity is derived from it, so changing it is how a design asks what a different storm level would cost.

## Picture it

Think of a town choosing how high to build its flood wall. Nobody builds for the worst flood physically possible; the town names a flood level to build for and keeps an evacuation plan for anything bigger. G3 is that named level for this spacecraft, and storms beyond it are met with procedures rather than with more wall.

## Guess first

{{guess A five-year mission should expect one big storm in its lifetime. Is that storm below, at, or above the G3 the design is built for? || Above, by two levels: the record says such a mission should expect G5. That gap is closed by operations, not by structure.}}

## Where it breaks

The record says a five-year mission should expect a storm two levels higher, G5; storms above G3 are meant to be flown through with procedures, not built for. The scale grades the worst three hours of a day while the design works in daily averages, and days whose worst three hours reach G3 range widely in daily average activity.

## Common misreading

That G3 is enough for a five-year mission. The storm such a mission should expect reaches G5; the gap is closed by operations, not by structure.
