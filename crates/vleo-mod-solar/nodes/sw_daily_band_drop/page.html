How far below its own rotation does a single day of F10.7 fall, at the declared confidence?

dF107_day_low = -pctl(F107 - movmean(F107, 27 d), 0.05) over the 2001 days ending at the window = 31.2722 sfu

Source noaa_swpc — NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center — solar and geomagnetic indices NOAA, retrieved 2026-09-01

The other tail of sw_daily_band_spread's sample, and a separate row because it is a separate number: the departures are not symmetric. The sun has a floor and no ceiling, so a day can rise further above its rotation than it can fall below it, and a design that mirrors one tail onto the other is assuming a shape the record does not have. It is the cold side, which sounds like the side nobody sizes against. It is not: a cold day is the low-drag case, and the low-drag case is what a propellant budget's LOWER bound and an aerodynamic control authority's worst case are made of. The hot day and the cold day both bound something.

sw_daily_band_spread takes the daily record, subtracts its own centred 27-day moving mean, and reads the 95th percentile of what is left. This reads the 5th percentile of the same sample, and publishes its magnitude.

That it is a second row rather than the same row negated is the whole point. The departures are skewed. Over the 2001 days before the declared window the upper tail reaches +34.23 and the lower only -31.27, a difference of about nine per cent, and the reason is physical rather than statistical: F10.7 has a floor near 65 sfu that the quiet sun sits on, so a rotation mean already near the floor has little room to fall and plenty to rise. Mirroring the upper tail would make the cold day about three sfu colder than the record supports, which is a denser atmosphere than the cold case actually is and a propellant budget bounded in the wrong place.

The magnitude is published rather than the signed percentile so that the row reads as a distance, its declared floor is zero, and the sign lives in the relation that consumes it rather than in the number. A row whose value is negative and whose consumers must remember to add rather than subtract is a row waiting to be used the wrong way round once.

  1. Take the daily record up to the day the window opens, and no further, and subtract the centred 27-day moving mean. The sample is identical to sw_daily_band_spread's — the same days, the same moving mean, one measurement.dev(t) = F107(t) - movmean(F107, 27)(t)
  2. Read the 5th percentile instead of the 95th. One call on one sorted sample gives both tails; this row is the lower one.pctl(dev, 0.05) = -31.2722 sfu
  3. Publish its magnitude, so the row is a distance with a floor of zero and the sign travels with the relation that uses it.dF107_day_low = -pctl(dev, 0.05) = +31.2722 sfu

31.27 sfu below the rotation the day sits in, against 34.23 above it. One day in twenty is at least that far down, on the same sample and by the same call.
