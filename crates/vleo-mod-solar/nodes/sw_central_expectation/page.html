explanation

1Say it simply

How bright the Sun will be, on average, over the years the mission actually flies. The Sun's activity rises and falls on a cycle of about eleven years, so when a mission flies matters as much as how long it flies: this row lays the shape past cycles followed over the mission's own dates, sized to the cycle it will meet, and averages it.

2Now the real thing

Where is F10.7 expected to be by the time the mission is there?

F107_central(T_e, L) = w*F107_today + (1-w)*mean_[T_e, T_e+L] A(t), w = exp(-L / 27 d), A(t) = amp(t) * R((t - T_max) mod P) sourced · noaa_swpc

Source noaa_swpc — NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center — solar and geomagnetic indices NOAA, retrieved 2026-09-01

The centre of the F10.7 design value; sw_uncertainty_growth supplies the spread around it and sw_f107_design adds the two together. Two things decide the answer and the row needs both: how far ahead it is being asked about, and WHEN. A mission through 2027-2032 flies the declining half of cycle 25 and meets a different sky from one through 2032-2037, and no single number can be right for both.

3Where the simple version breaks

It is an average over the whole mission, and a spacecraft meets its worst stretch, not its average: five years from the declared epoch average about 90 sfu while opening near 105 and ending near 73. The highest stretch is sw_window_peak_level's answer, not this one. And the shape rests on two completed cycles, with the next cycle's size taken as the mean of their peaks — which is the one thing cycles do not reliably repeat.

Assumptions, and where each stops holding
Two completed cycles is the whole sample, and eleven of the ninety-three grid points rest on one of them
fails when the record spans cycles 23, 24 and the incomplete 25, so the shape R is a mean of TWO curves and its spread between them is not published by this row. Where the two cycles' differing lengths leave only one of them covering a point — eleven of ninety-three, near the wrap — the value is that one cycle's shape rather than an average. Two cycles cannot establish that a shape repeats; they can only establish what the last two did, and this row says the next one resembles them because that is the best the record supports, not because it is known.
Outside cycle 25 the amplitude is the mean of TWO completed cycles, and their spread is a factor of 1.41
fails when cycle 23 peaked at 226.8 sfu and cycle 24 at 160.9, so the 193.9 this row uses for every future cycle is the midpoint of two numbers that differ by 41%. A window reaching past about 2030 is reading a level whose size is that average, and if the next cycle runs like cycle 23 the answer is 17% low, if like cycle 24 it is 17% high. That is an honest estimate rather than a repeat of the current cycle, which is what this row used to do, but two cycles cannot support an uncertainty on it and none is published. The row does not know, and does not claim to know, which kind of cycle comes next.
The answer is a window MEAN, so it understates the early years of a long mission
fails when a five-year mission opening at the declared epoch averages 90.4 sfu, but its first ninety days average 104.6 and it falls to 73.2 by the end of the window — a spread of 39 sfu inside one number. Drag is not linear in flux and a vehicle does not average its propellant over five years, so a design whose sizing case is its worst sustained period is reading the wrong statistic here. This row publishes a centre because it is a centre; the maximum of the analogue over the window is a different number and nothing publishes it yet.

This moves the row AWAY from the MATLAB tool, deliberately

Outside 60 … 400 the row refuses rather than answers: the answer is a weighted blend of today's F10.7 and a window mean of the analogue, so it cannot leave the interval between them. The analogue is bounded below by 0.324026 x 193.8580 = 62.8 sfu, its smallest shape on its smallest amplitude, and env_f107 by 60 because below 60 sfu has never been observed; the blend therefore floors at 60; env_f107's upper bound is 400, above which the exospheric temperature relation is extrapolated past the largest recorded daily value. The analogue cannot exceed cycle 25's own 81-day peak of 225.1 sfu, and a blend cannot exceed its larger input, so this bound catches a broken weight or a broken table rather than an extreme sky.

4Common wrong idea

That the long-term average of the record, about 115 sfu, is the right centre for any mission. It is right only for a mission longer than a whole cycle. For a five-year mission on the declining half of cycle 25 the record puts it 26 per cent high, and the legacy tool's frozen rotation forecast 74 per cent high.

explanation

A design needs one number for the flux it will meet, and which number is right depends on how far ahead the question is asked AND on where in the solar cycle the answer lands. Today's flux is excellent information about tomorrow and worthless about next decade, because the active region that makes it informative lives about one solar rotation. So the estimator has to hand over from persistence to a climatology.

The question this row used to get wrong is WHICH climatology. Handing over to the record's unconditional mean says a mission flying through solar minimum and one flying through maximum should be designed to the same flux, which the record flatly denies: its F10.7 runs from 64 to 343 sfu and the difference between those is almost entirely which part of a cycle you are in. The cycle is the most predictable thing about solar activity at a lead of years — far more predictable than the level itself — so a climatology that throws the cycle away is discarding the one piece of information a long-lead forecast actually has.

Each line below is derived from the one before, ending at the relation — which is sourced · noaa_swpc.

  1. Two estimators are available and they fail at opposite ends. Persistence — assume the flux stays where it is — is nearly exact at a lead of one day and absurd at a lead of one year. Climatology — assume the flux is typical — is useless at a lead of one day and the best available answer at a lead of one decade. So the estimator is a lead-weighted blend of the two, and the timescale is the synodic solar rotation because that is how long an active region faces the Earth.F107_central = w*F107_today + (1-w)*C, w = exp(-L / 27 d)
  2. The climatology C is where the work is. "Typical" is not a single number unless the question has no date attached, and this question has one: the mission epoch is published at the system layer and this row can read it.
  3. Align the completed cycles on their MAXIMA rather than their starts. Cycle length varies — 11.88 years for cycle 23 against 11.00 for cycle 24 — so aligning on starts puts one cycle's maximum against the other's decline and averages a peak with a slope. The study's own explorer says the same thing about its start-aligned view in as many words: it smears the peaks.u = t - T_max, measured forward from each cycle's own maximum
  4. Normalise each cycle by its own amplitude before averaging, so that the shape and the size are separated. What repeats between cycles is the shape; the size does not, and cycle 24 peaked at 160.6 sfu against cycle 23's 226.5. Averaging unnormalised would produce a curve that is neither cycle.R(u) = mean_c [ F107A_c(T_max,c + u) / max_t F107A_c(t) ]
  5. Both the numerator and the denominator are the 81-day centred mean, which is the standard F10.7 averaging window and the one sw_f107_81day already uses. Normalising a lightly smoothed value by a heavily smoothed peak would let the ratio exceed one; measured the same way on both sides, R is bounded by its own construction and runs 0.325 to 1.000.
  6. Put the shape back on a size, and WHICH size is the question the wrap forces. Inside cycle 25 the size has been measured: its 81-day peak is 225.1 sfu on 2024-09-04. Outside it the size has not happened yet, and the honest estimate is not a repeat of this cycle but the mean of the cycles that have finished — 193.9 sfu, from cycle 23's 226.8 and cycle 24's 160.9. Shape repeats between cycles; size is the thing that does not.amp(t) = 225.1358 sfu inside cycle 25, 193.8580 sfu outside it
  7. The modulo is what makes the row answerable over its whole declared domain. The epoch may be any date from 2018 to 2040 and the mission up to fifteen years, so the window can end well beyond any observed cycle; wrapping at the mean completed-cycle length of 4178 days says a future cycle resembles the average of the last two. The amplitude handover above is what keeps that claim to the shape alone, and the price is a step in the answer at half a period either side of the maximum. The step is not smoothed away: it marks the boundary between a cycle whose size was measured and cycles whose size was assumed, and it falls at the cycle minimum where it is smallest.the handover sits at knot 47 of 94, exactly half a period from the maximum
  8. Average over the window rather than evaluating at its end. The end of a five-year mission opening in 2027 sits near solar minimum, so an endpoint answer would size the design for the quietest sky it meets and miss the first three years entirely. The mean is also what the study's own driver construction takes.C = (1/L) * integral over [T_e, T_e + L] of A(t) dt

Reading the answer

At every lead a mission can ask about this row IS the climatology, because the persistence weight exp(-L/27 d) is 0.00114 at the shortest lead the declared range allows. What the answer now carries is the epoch: the same five-year mission returns 86.8 sfu opening in 2027, 115.0 sfu opening in 2018 and 144.9 sfu opening in late 2032. That spread is the content of the change.

The answer is a MEAN over the mission window, not the flux on any day in it. A five-year mission opening in 2027 averages 86.8 sfu while its first ninety days average 104.6 and its last fall to 73.2, so this number understates the early years of a long mission and overstates its end. sw_window_peak_level publishes the maximum over the same window for a design that must survive its worst sustained period rather than its average one. A design sized by its worst sustained period wants the maximum of the analogue over the window, which this row does not publish.

One property is worth checking because it is what keeps the change anchored: as the window grows past a full cycle the mean settles near the record's own unconditional climatology — 105.7 sfu at fifteen years against the record's 114.8. It settles BELOW it rather than on it, and for a stated reason: cycles beyond this one are given the mean amplitude of the completed cycles, 193.9 sfu, where the record's own mean is lifted by cycle 23 at 226.8. The long-run answer is therefore the record's level minus the assumption that the future is average rather than strong.

pub fn evaluate(today: Ratio, lead: Time, epoch: Time) -> Result<Ratio, Fault> {
    // ---- HOLE 1 : average the amplitude-scaled cycle analogue over the mission window -> Ratio
    // The shape, the period, both amplitudes and the handover between them are
    // env::solar_cycle_analogue's; this row composes the window mean of it.
    let climo: Ratio = Ratio::new(env::solar_cycle_analogue_mean(
        epoch.days(),
        epoch.days() + lead.days(),
    ));
    // ---- end HOLE 1
    // ---- HOLE 2 : weight today's flux against that window climatology by the lead, one solar rotation as the timescale -> Ratio
    // One synodic solar rotation, prf_design's own timescale for persistence. At
    // every lead the declared input range allows this weight is below 0.0012, so
    // the term is inert and kept only because the relation is the study's.
    const TAU_DAYS: f64 = 27.0;
    let w: f64 = pmath::exp(-lead.days() / TAU_DAYS);
    let central: Ratio = Ratio::new(w * today.get() + (1.0 - w) * climo.get());
    // ---- end HOLE 2

    // generated · the declared domain of this node's own answer. The
    // reason travels with the guard, because a guard whose reason is not
    // written down gets deleted by the next person who finds it awkward.
    let answer: Ratio = central;
    if !answer.is_finite() {
        return Err(Fault::Degenerate { node: NODE_ID, field: "F107_central", reason: "the computation produced a value that is not a number" });
    }
    if answer.get() < 60.0 {
        return Err(Fault::OutOfDomain { node: NODE_ID, field: "F107_central", value: answer.get(), bound: 60.0, edge: Edge::Lower, unit: Ratio::UNIT, reason: "the answer is a weighted blend of today's F10.7 and a window mean of the analogue, so it cannot leave the interval between them. The analogue is bounded below by 0.324026 x 193.8580 = 62.8 sfu, its smallest shape on its smallest amplitude, and env_f107 by 60 because below 60 sfu has never been observed; the blend therefore floors at 60" });
    }
    if answer.get() > 400.0 {
        return Err(Fault::OutOfDomain { node: NODE_ID, field: "F107_central", value: answer.get(), bound: 400.0, edge: Edge::Upper, unit: Ratio::UNIT, reason: "env_f107's upper bound is 400, above which the exospheric temperature relation is extrapolated past the largest recorded daily value. The analogue cannot exceed cycle 25's own 81-day peak of 225.1 sfu, and a blend cannot exceed its larger input, so this bound catches a broken weight or a broken table rather than an extreme sky" });
    }
    Ok(answer)
}
