Where is F10.7 expected to be by the time the mission is there?

F107_central(L) = w*F107_today + (1-w)*114.8437, w = exp(-L / 27 d)

Source noaa_swpc — NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center — solar and geomagnetic indices NOAA, retrieved 2026-09-01

The centre of the F10.7 design value; sw_uncertainty_growth supplies the spread around it and sw_f107_design adds the two together. Today's flux is worth something at a lead of days and nothing at a lead of years, so this is the one number that has to know how far ahead it is being asked about.

A design needs one number for the flux it will meet, and which number is right depends entirely on how far ahead the question is being asked. Today's flux is excellent information about tomorrow, decent information about next week, and worthless information about next decade — because the physical thing that makes it informative, an active region facing the Earth, lives for about one solar rotation and then is gone. So the honest central value is not persistence and not climatology but a handover between them, and the whole content of the relation is the shape of that handover and the timescale it runs on.

  1. Two estimators are available and they fail at opposite ends. Persistence — assume the flux stays where it is — is nearly exact at a lead of one day and absurd at a lead of one year. Climatology — assume the flux is typical — is useless at a lead of one day and the best available answer at a lead of one decade.
  2. So the estimator has to be a weighted blend whose weight depends on the lead, running from all persistence at zero lead to all climatology at long lead.F107_central(L) = w(L) * F107_today + (1 - w(L)) * C, w(0) = 1, w(inf) = 0
  3. The shape chosen is exponential decay. That is a decision and prf_design cites no source for it: several forms satisfy the two end conditions above, and this one says the information in today's flux decays at a constant fractional rate.w(L) = exp(-L / tau)
  4. The timescale is not fitted, and that is what makes it defensible. 27 days is the synodic solar rotation — the period over which an active region carries round to the far side and stops facing the Earth — so the relation says the memory in today's flux dies over about one turn of the Sun.tau = 27 d, one synodic solar rotation
  5. The climatology is the record's mean F10.7 over its 10316 observed days, measured outside this crate on the pinned bundle. It is the UNCONDITIONAL mean: the average over every part of every cycle, which is not the expected flux at any particular date.C = 114.8437 sfu
  6. At which point the arithmetic settles the row's character. Half a year is 6.8 rotations, so the persistence weight is already at the fourth decimal place, and for any mission lead this row returns the climatology. Everything interesting about the relation happens in a region of lead that nothing in this tree asks about.w(182.6 d) = exp(-6.76) = 0.00116

Reading the answer

At every lead a mission can ask about, this row IS the climatology. The weight on today's flux is exp(-L/27 d), which at the shortest lead the declared input range allows — half a year — is 0.00114, and by one year is 1.3e-6. So the persistence term is arithmetically inert here and the answer is 114.8437 to four decimal places for any mission. It is kept because it is the study's relation and because removing it would make the row silently wrong for a short-lead use this tree does not currently make. The number it hands over to is the record's unconditional mean, which is the average of maxima and minima together; the sheet's first assumption is now a pending decision rather than a missing input, and it is the most consequential open item on this node.

Guards

  • F107_central ≥ 60 — the answer is a weighted blend of today's F10.7 and the record's mean, so it can never leave the interval between them. env_f107's own lower bound is 60 because below 60 sfu has never been observed and the fit has no support there; the same floor applies to a blend of it
  • F107_central ≤ 400 — env_f107's upper bound is 400, above which the exospheric temperature relation is extrapolated past the largest recorded daily value. A blend cannot exceed its larger input, so this bound catches a broken weight rather than an extreme sky

The reason travels with the guard. A guard whose reason is not written down gets deleted by the next person who finds it awkward.
