The same construction as the F10.7 daily spread: subtract the record's own centred 27-day moving mean from each day and read the 95th percentile of what is left, over the 2001 days ending at the window.

For Ap the number is larger than its own centre — 15.00 against a centre of 22.10 — and that is the physics rather than an error. F10.7 varies smoothly as active regions rotate into view; Ap is quiet for weeks and then a coronal mass ejection arrives and it is 100 for a day. A within-rotation spread comparable to the level itself is what a burst process looks like when you measure it this way.

  1. Take the daily Ap record up to the day the window opens, and no further.hist = Ap over the 2001 days ending at the window
  2. Subtract the centred 27-day moving mean from each day.dev(t) = Ap(t) - movmean(Ap, 27)(t)
  3. Read the 95th percentile of the departures. The 5th is sw_ap_daily_band_drop's own row: at 10.59 against this 15.00 the two tails are forty-two per cent apart, because Ap is truncated at zero.pctl(dev, 0.95) = +15.0019 · pctl(dev, 0.05) = -10.5889

Reading the answer

15.0019, against the study's 14.8519 — an absolute difference of 0.15, the same ORDER as the 0.083 the F10.7 row differs by. Ap is simply a smaller number, so the same convention difference reads as 1.01 per cent where F10.7's reads as 0.24, which is why the grid beside this row is declared at 2 per cent and its F10.7 twin at 1.

The cold tail is sw_ap_daily_band_drop at 10.59, forty-two per cent shallower than this. That gap is the largest asymmetry in the subsystem and it is physical: Ap floors at zero, so a rotation cannot fall further than its own mean.
