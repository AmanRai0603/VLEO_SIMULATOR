The lead the forecast-verification rows are evaluated at. sw_forecast_skill and sw_forecast_bias both answer 'at what lead', and this is the lead worth asking about: the far end of the published window, where the outlook is weakest and a design reading it is most exposed. 3Where the simple version breaks

Assumptions, and where each stops holding
The product is 27 days long and this row is 26, so it does not answer 'how far ahead does the outlook reach'
fails when somebody reads it as the window length. 899 of 1281 issues span exactly 27 days and the synodic solar rotation is about 27.3 days, so 27 is the honest answer to that question and this row is not asking it. It is asking where the outlook is weakest among the leads the record can verify, and the 27th lead cannot be verified because only 192 of 1281 issues place a row there. If a row is ever needed for the window length it is a second row, not this one.
One lead, and the skill of the outlook is strongly lead-dependent
fails when measured on this record the outlook's skill against persistence rises from +0.0685 at lead 1 to a peak of +0.4375 at lead 9, falls back through +0.0177 at lead 23, and goes negative at 24, 25 and 26. So a single lead cannot characterise the product: 26 is the pessimistic end and a row evaluated there says nothing about the useful middle. It is chosen because a design wants to know how bad the far edge is, not how good the centre is.
Skill is measured against persistence defined as the last observation STRICTLY BEFORE the issue date
fails when the baseline is allowed the observation on the issue date itself. That is the lead-0 target for the 719 issues that index from 0, so it hands persistence an answer the forecaster did not have, and it flatters the baseline enormously: the same arithmetic then reports the lead-1 skill as -1.3138 instead of +0.0685, and the outlook appears to lose to persistence at leads 1 through 4 when it does not. One choice of baseline, and the sign of the short-lead conclusion changes. This row's own first version carried the leaky figure and said the outlook loses at short leads; it does not.

Outside 1 … 26 d the row refuses rather than answers: a lead of less than a day is not a forecast the published outlook makes; its first verifiable row is lead 1, present for all 1281 issues; 26 is the last lead in the published window that the record can verify, because lead_days is indexed two ways in the same column and only the 1-based minority reaches 27. At 26 the sample is 868 issues drawn from both conventions; at 27 it is 192 drawn from one, and the answer there has a different sign from each of its neighbours. A larger value would be asking the verification rows about a cell whose contents are a property of the indexing rather than of the forecast.

explanation

Two rows in this subsystem verify the published short-term outlook, and both of them answer 'at what lead'. A lead therefore has to be declared somewhere, and choosing it is not a formality: the outlook's skill rises, peaks and goes negative across its own window, so a single lead can make the product look useful or useless depending on where it is put. This row picks the pessimistic end, because what a design needs to know about a forecast is how bad its far edge is rather than how good its centre is. The whole difficulty is that the far edge of the PRODUCT and the far edge of what the RECORD CAN VERIFY are not the same lead.

Each line below is derived from the one before, ending at the relation — which is sourced · noaa_swpc.

  1. The published outlook is a 27-day product, so the obvious choice of far-edge lead is 27. Counting the issues shows why that is not available: of 1281 issues, 900 carry 27 rows and 349 carry 14.
  2. Worse than uneven length, the column is indexed two ways in the same file. Among the 899 issues spanning 27 days, 719 index their rows from lead 0 and 175 from lead 1 — so 'lead 27' exists only for the 1-based minority.issues reaching lead 27 = 192 of 1281
  3. That makes any statistic at lead 27 a property of the indexing rather than of the forecast, and it shows: the skill at 27 has a different sign from each of its neighbours. At lead 26 the sample is 868 issues drawn from BOTH conventions, which is a cell whose contents mean something.
  4. So the declared lead is 26 — the last lead in the published window the record can verify — and the upper bound is set there rather than at 27 so that nothing can quietly ask about the unverifiable cell.L_short = 26 d
  5. One further choice sits behind every number quoted above and it is the baseline. Persistence here is the last observation STRICTLY BEFORE the issue date. Allowing it the observation ON the issue date hands it a lead-0 target for the 719 issues that index from zero — a value the forecaster did not have — and the lead-1 skill then reads -1.314 instead of +0.069, making the outlook appear to lose to persistence through lead 4 when it does not. This row's own first version carried the leaky figure.

Reading the answer

It is not the window length. 899 of 1281 issues span exactly 27 days and the synodic solar rotation is about 27.3 days, so 27 is the honest answer to 'how far ahead does the outlook reach' — and this row is not asking that. If a row is ever needed for the window length it is a second row. A single lead also cannot characterise the product: skill runs from +0.069 at lead 1 to +0.438 at lead 9 and back to -0.022 at lead 26, so a verification at 26 says nothing about the useful middle.
