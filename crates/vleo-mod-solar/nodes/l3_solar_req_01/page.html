A requirement is not a prediction and not a capability: it is a commitment, and its purpose is to be checkable against something measured. This row commits the design to surviving a solar flux, and the checking partner is l3_solar_ach_01, which reports what the record actually presents. The pair is the closure, and the whole reason a requirement lives on its own row — rather than being inferred from the design value — is that a commitment which silently tracked the design could never be violated and would therefore never be a requirement.

Reading the answer

It is a ceiling on the DRIVER and says nothing about what the driver does to the vehicle. F10.7 is an index of solar radio flux; what a spacecraft feels is the density that flux produces at its altitude, through a model this subsystem does not own. A design that meets F10.7 <= 250 and is sized with the wrong drag coefficient has met this requirement and will still deorbit early. The margin also has no derivation behind it: 250 is a round number above what the chain then achieved, chosen so the closure passes with visible room rather than by a hair, and a design that wants the margin to mean something specific should replace it with a number that has a derivation. The achieved side has since fallen from 228.14 to 200.14 — sw_central_expectation stopped using the record's unconditional mean — so the headroom this round number happens to give has widened from 9.6 to 24.9 per cent. The requirement has NOT been re-derived to match, and whether it should come down is a decision rather than an arithmetic consequence.
