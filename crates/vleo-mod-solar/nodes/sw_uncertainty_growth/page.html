sw_central_expectation says where F10.7 is heading and is a median-like quantity, exceeded about half the time. This row supplies the other half of a design value: how wrong that centre can be by the time the mission is there. The quantity is a percentile of the CHANGE over a lead rather than a percentile of the value, because what a design is exposed to is the drift away from what was assumed when it was sized. And the interesting result is that the answer is not monotone in lead, which is a fact about the solar cycle and the reason this row is a table rather than a curve.

  1. Define the quantity as the change in flux over a lead, not the flux: for every pair of observed days L apart, how far did F10.7 move? The distribution of that difference is what a design's exposure to being wrong actually looks like.dF107(L) = F107(t + L) - F107(t)
  2. Bound it by a measured percentile rather than by a multiple of a standard deviation. The distribution is strongly asymmetric — the flux is bounded below near 64 sfu and has a long upper tail to 343 — so a symmetric interval would be wrong on both sides.dF107_p95(L) = 95th percentile of dF107 over all observed pairs at lead L
  3. Only pairs where BOTH days were observed are used, and that is a deliberate departure from the study. prf_design builds a full daily grid and fills the record's 273 absent days by linear interpolation, which gives those days no variability at all and understates every difference spanning them.
  4. The departure has a measured size and a direction. Computed both ways on this record, the interpolated grid gives a p95 between 1.0 and 2.3 sfu LOWER across leads from 7 to 1826 days — a design band narrower than the record supports, in the unsafe direction. This row is therefore expected to disagree with prf_design by about that much, which is why a parity grid beside it would have recorded a disagreement rather than a match.
  5. Now the result that decides the relation's form. The measured p95 is NOT monotone in lead: it rises to +114 sfu at four years, falls to +66 at ten to eleven, and rises again to +115 at fifteen.
  6. That shape is the eleven-year cycle showing through, and it is exactly what the cycle predicts. A lead of about half a cycle is the lead most likely to land on the opposite phase from where it started, so the change is largest there; a lead of about a full cycle returns to a similar phase, so the change comes back down.
  7. Which rules out every monotone form, log-linear included. A monotone fit would report roughly +90 sfu at an eleven-year lead where the record says +66, overstating the band by a third at exactly the lead a long mission cares about. So the seventeen measured leads are tabulated and the interpolation between them is this row's only choice.

Guards
