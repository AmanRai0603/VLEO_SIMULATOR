What Kp does this daily planetary Ap mean?

Kp = piecewise_linear(ap_28 -> kp_28, clamp(Ap, 0, 400))

Source iaga_kp_ap — Bartels, J. — The standardized index Ks and the planetary index Kp, with the ap equivalent-amplitude table IATME Bulletin 12b, IUGG Publication Office, 1949; the 28-point table as published by IAGA and distributed by GFZ Potsdam

The atmosphere model wants Kp; the design product carries Ap. This is that conversion and only that — the published table, applied as published. What flows in is the design storm from sw_storm_return_level, so what flows out is the Kp of that storm and not of an average day. The bias the table carries when a daily mean is fed to a three-hourly scale is measured separately, in sw_kp_slot_bias, and is not corrected here.

Two scales describe the same geomagnetic disturbance and neither can be dropped. Kp is a range index: observatories report how far the field wandered during a three-hour interval, graded in thirds from 0 to 9, and the grading is quasi-logarithmic because the field's excursions span orders of magnitude. ap is the LINEAR equivalent of the same grade, in nanotesla, and exists so that disturbances can be averaged — you cannot meaningfully average a logarithmic index. So designs and archives carry Ap while atmosphere models ask for Kp, and the correspondence between them is not a formula anyone derives: it is a table IAGA publishes, 28 pairs, and this row applies it as published.

  1. Kp is a grade, not a measurement. Each observatory reports the range its field swept in a three-hour interval, converts that range to a 0-to-9 grade on a quasi-logarithmic scale, and the planetary index is the average of those grades — which is why it comes in thirds.
  2. A logarithmic grade cannot be averaged or summed, and both are needed constantly: a daily figure, a monthly mean, an integral over a storm. So ap exists as the linear amplitude each Kp grade stands for, in nanotesla, and Ap is the daily mean of the eight ap values.
  3. The correspondence between the two is defined rather than derived. IAGA publishes 28 pairs — one for each third of a Kp unit — and ap grows roughly geometrically along them, from 0 at Kp 0 to 400 at Kp 9. That table IS the definition, so this row's only job is to read it in the ap-to-Kp direction.ap = 0, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 12, ... 236, 300, 400 for Kp = 0, 1/3, 2/3, ... 9
  4. Between two tabulated points the answer is this row's choice and not the source's, because the published scale is discrete. Straight lines in ap are used. Since ap grows geometrically, interpolating in ap rather than in its logarithm understates Kp inside a bin — and the largest departure between the two conventions over the whole domain is 0.017 Kp, which bounds the size of the arbitrariness.worst |linear - log interpolation| = 0.017 Kp, in the 2-to-3 bin
  5. Now the consequential part, which is not about the table but about what is fed into it. Because ap is geometric in Kp, the inverse Kp(ap) is CONCAVE, and concavity has a direction by Jensen's inequality: the table applied to an average reads higher than the average of the table applied to each value.Kp(mean of ap_i) >= mean of Kp(ap_i) for concave Kp
  6. So feeding a daily mean Ap into a three-hourly scale has two predictable errors with OPPOSITE signs, and the record confirms both. Over 10297 days carrying an Ap and all eight Kp, the table reads high against the 24-hour mean Kp by 0.083 (median) and low against the daily peak by 1.000; on the disturbed days that size a drag design (Ap >= 48, 131 of them) those become 0.397 high and 1.606 low.
  7. Neither is corrected here, and that is a structural choice rather than an omission. A correction depends on which slot the caller wants, and a node answers one question: this one answers what the published table says. sw_kp_slot_bias measures the offsets and publishes the peak-slot one for a consumer to add.

Reading the answer

The answer is the Kp of whatever Ap was handed in, with no correction applied. Two things follow. First, the input here is a DAILY MEAN Ap and the table is defined for a three-hourly ap, so the answer sits above the mean of the day's eight Kp and a full Kp unit below the day's worst slot — sw_kp_slot_bias measures both and publishes the second, and a consumer that needs the peak adds it. Second, the table clamps: above ap 400 every storm returns exactly 9, so a scenario multiplier applied to a disturbed day can silently flatten the largest storms into the same number as merely severe ones.

which is the relation this node states: Kp = piecewise_linear(ap_28 -> kp_28, clamp(Ap, 0, 400))

Guards

  • Kp ≥ 0 — Kp is defined on 0..9 and the table's first point is Kp 0 at ap 0; a negative index is a sign error, not a quiet day
  • Kp ≤ 9 — Kp is defined on 0..9 and the table's last point is Kp 9 at ap 400. This is the same guard env_kp carries, and it catches an ap value reaching a consumer that wanted Kp

The reason travels with the guard. A guard whose reason is not written down gets deleted by the next person who finds it awkward.
