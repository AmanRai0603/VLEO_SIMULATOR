Kp slot bias, 24-hour mean

sw_kp_mean_bias · computed · owner environment · tier A · state published

By how much does the published table over-read the 24-hour MEAN Kp at this Ap?

dKp_mean(Ap) = piecewise_linear(ap_bin_centres -> measured_medians, Ap)

Source noaa_swpc — NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center — solar and geomagnetic indices NOAA, retrieved 2026-09-01

The pair to sw_kp_slot_bias, and the half of the pair a thermospheric model actually asks for more often. DTM2020_Oper takes Kp in two senses — akp(1) a single three-hourly value, akp(3) the mean of the day's eight — and the published scale answers neither, because it is defined for a three-hourly ap while the design product carries a daily mean. sw_kp_slot_bias measures the peak-slot gap and this measures the mean-slot one. They have opposite signs, so applying the wrong one doubles the error instead of removing it.

Reading a daily mean Ap through a scale built for three-hourly ap is wrong in two directions at once, and which direction depends on what the reader wanted. Its sibling handles the case where the reader wanted the day's worst slot. This handles the case where the reader wanted the day's average, which is the more common ask and the one that looks least like an error: the number arrives already labelled as a daily mean, so nothing about it invites a correction.

Jensen's inequality settles the direction before any measurement. Kp is concave in ap, so averaging eight Kp values cannot exceed the Kp of their averaged ap — the table reads HIGH against the 24-hour mean, and reads high by more on disturbed days, which are the days a drag design is sized by. What the inequality cannot give is the size, because the size depends on how unevenly a real day spreads its disturbance across eight slots. That is a property of storms, not of the table, so it is measured.

  1. Define the quantity as the gap between what the day actually averaged and what a design reads for it: the mean of the day's eight three-hourly Kp, minus the table's reading of the day's mean Ap.dKp_mean(day) = mean_8 Kp(day) - Kp_table(Ap(day))
  2. Its sign is known in advance, and it is the opposite of the sibling's. Kp is concave in ap, so by Jensen's inequality the mean of the eight Kp cannot exceed the Kp of their mean ap. The difference is therefore at or below zero wherever the published Ap really is the mean of the day's eight ap.mean_8 Kp(ap_i) <= Kp(mean_8 ap_i) = Kp_table(Ap_daily)
  3. It is measured as a function of Ap rather than as one number, because the unevenness grows with activity: a quiet day is uniformly quiet across its eight slots, while a storm concentrates into a few. Nine bins, prf_ap2kp's own edges, and the median in each.edges = 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45, 70, 110, 400
  4. The medians deepen with Ap as the concavity argument predicts, from -0.125 at Ap 7.5 to -0.487 in the top bin — and unlike the peak-slot offset they do not turn back at the top. So the correction a design reads grows steadily with the disturbance it is designing for, which is the behaviour that makes ignoring it worst exactly where it matters most.
  5. One bin contradicts the inequality. The quietest, Ap 0 to 5, measures +0.042 — the table reads LOW there rather than high. Two things could produce that and this row settles neither: the record publishes SWPC's ESTIMATED planetary Ap rather than the exact mean of the day's eight ap, so the quantity Jensen applies to is not quite the quantity in the column; and at the quiet end both scales are coarse next to an offset of one twenty-fourth of a Kp unit. It is carried through as measured rather than clipped to zero, because clipping it would hide a disagreement between the record and the argument this node rests on.
  6. Between bin centres the answer is interpolated and at the ends it is held rather than extrapolated, which is prf_ap2kp's own rule stated explicitly: hold the end bins, never extrapolate.

Reading the answer

A correction to be ADDED, and it is almost everywhere negative: the answer subtracts. Its magnitude is small next to its sibling's — a tenth of a Kp unit where the peak-slot gap is well over one — because an average is much closer to the table's reading than an extreme is. That smallness is not a reason to skip it, since the whole point of a bias is that it does not average out over a mission.

It is a median per bin, so it recovers the typical day at that Ap and misses any individual one. A design needing a worst case at a given Ap needs a percentile of the offset rather than its median, and this row does not publish the spread.

Guards

  • dKp_mean ≥ -1 — the deepest measured bin median is -0.487 and the relation is a table that clamps at its ends, so no input can produce less. -1.0 is therefore unreachable by this relation and exists to catch a broken table rather than an extreme sky
