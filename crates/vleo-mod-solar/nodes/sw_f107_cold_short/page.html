How cold can a single day of F10.7 be inside the mission window?

F107_cold_short = F107_cold_long - dF107_day_low

Source noaa_swpc — NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center — solar and geomagnetic indices NOAA, retrieved 2026-09-01

The coldest of the five scenarios, and the one a drag-based capability is weakest at. Its hot twin, sw_f107_design_short, is the worst single day upward; this is the worst single day downward, and the two are NOT mirror images even though the rows look identical bar a sign. They are not mirrors because the two terms behave differently. The rotation band is symmetric — both edges are 1.28 standard deviations from the centre. The daily term is not, at any level: at a rotation of 70 sfu the tails are 4.63 up and 4.39 down, at 145 they are 34.61 and 32.39, at 210 they are 46.93 and 40.78. And the two days are not even read off the same point of the table, because the hot day rides on the hot mean and the cold day on the cold mean. At this window that is 20.07 added at 104.07 against 4.39 taken off at 69.63.

The two spreads are of different things and they stack rather than combine. sw_mean_band_spread is how wrong the pattern is about the LEVEL a rotation sits at — a forecast error. sw_daily_band_drop is how far a day falls below the rotation it belongs to — the sun's own variability, measured on days that already happened. A day is low because its rotation is low AND because it is a low day within that rotation, so the design level for a cold day is the sustained cold level with the daily drop taken off.

That is a deliberately conservative construction and the sheet says so. Stacking two one-sided percentiles gives a bound further out than either — roughly the 99th rather than the 95th if the two were independent and normal — so the published day is rarer than the confidence attached to it suggests. The study does the same thing and the port follows it.

Two things differ from the hot twin, not one. Which tail of the daily sample is used — and the tails differ by six to fifteen per cent depending on the level, because F10.7 has a floor and no ceiling. And WHERE ON THE TABLE it is read, because sw_daily_band_drop is evaluated at the cold sustained level and sw_daily_band_spread at the hot one. At this window those are 69.63 and 104.07, and the two answers are 4.39 and 20.07: a factor of four and a half apart, from one measurement read at two places.

  1. Take the sustained cold level: the centre less 1.28 standard deviations of the rotation-forecast residual.F107_cold_long = sw_f107_cold_long
  2. Take how far a day falls below its own rotation, at the declared confidence AND AT THE LEVEL THAT ROTATION SITS AT. The LOW tail, read at the COLD sustained level: 4.39 at 69.63 sfu, against the high tail's 20.07 at 104.07.dF107_day_low = sw_daily_band_drop(F107_cold_long) = 4.3870 sfu at a sustained 69.63
  3. Subtract it. The two are stacked rather than combined in quadrature, which is what the study does and is conservative — a stacked pair of one-sided percentiles is a rarer day than either confidence describes.F107_cold_short = F107_cold_long - dF107_day_low

Reading the answer

Fed the study's own two numbers the arithmetic is exact: its coldmean 141.0544028132 minus its own daily drop of 31.3333333333 is 109.7210694798, which is the coldday scenario it published to the last digit. That is what the parity grid beside this sheet checks, and it is a check on the RELATION.

THIS ROW USED TO REFUSE, AND WHY IT NO LONGER DOES IS WORTH READING. Its own chain does not reach 109: sw_central_expectation gives 86.8497 sfu at the declared epoch rather than the study's 158.33, so the sustained cold level is 69.6281. Subtract the drop this row USED to be handed — a single 31.2722, measured over days whose rotations averaged 136.63 sfu — and the answer was 38.3559, below the guard at 60 and below anything ever observed. The row returned OutOfDomain and the seam refused with it.

The guard was right and the construction was wrong. A drop measured at a rotation level of 137 does not apply at a rotation level of 70, because the within-rotation departure scales hard with the level: over the record it runs 4.39 at 70 sfu to 40.78 at 210, a factor of nine. sw_daily_band_drop is now a table conditioned on that level, and at 69.63 it gives 4.39. This row publishes 65.2411, which clears the floor by five and is a genuinely quiet day.

What that cost, and it is not nothing: a seven-fold change in one term of a published design level. The old number was not a conservative margin, it was an answer to a question about a different sun. The two remaining candidate improvements are still upstream and still open — a sigma measured for the window's own phase rather than pooled across the cycle, and empirical percentiles of the residuals in place of a normal multiplier on a skewed sample.
