The cold tail of sw_ap_daily_band_spread's sample, and a separate row because Ap's two tails are nothing like each other: 15.00 up against 10.59 down. The geomagnetic index is the most skewed quantity in this subsystem, because it floors hard at zero and has no ceiling at all. The cold day is the low-drag case, and on the Ap side it is also the quiet-sky case a magnetometer, a magnetorquer sizing and an aerodynamic control authority are bounded by from below.

The measurement is sw_ap_daily_band_spread's, read from the other end. Take the daily Ap record up to the day the window opens, subtract its own centred 27-day moving mean, sort what is left and read the 5th percentile instead of the 95th. One sample, one moving mean, two tails.

The asymmetry is the reason this is a row. On F10.7 the two tails differ by nine per cent; on Ap they differ by forty-two. 15.00 up, 10.59 down. Ap is bounded below at 0 and a quiet day really does sit there, so a rotation whose mean is 20 cannot fall more than 20 and can rise without limit — the distribution is not merely skewed, it is truncated. Mirroring the hot tail onto the cold side would put the cold day at Ap 4.5 where the record supports 6.9, which is a quieter sky than has been observed for that rotation level and a thinner atmosphere than the cold case actually has.

It is published as a magnitude for the same reason as its F10.7 twin: the row is then a distance with a declared floor of zero, and the sign belongs to the relation that consumes it rather than to the number.

  1. Take the daily Ap record up to the day the window opens, and no further, and subtract the centred 27-day moving mean. The sample is identical to sw_ap_daily_band_spread's — the same days, the same moving mean, one measurement.dev(t) = Ap(t) - movmean(Ap, 27)(t)
  2. Read the 5th percentile instead of the 95th.pctl(dev, 0.05) = -10.5889
  3. Publish its magnitude, so the row is a distance with a floor of zero and the sign travels with the relation that uses it.dAp_day_low = -pctl(dev, 0.05) = +10.5889
