What F10.7 should this design be sized to?

F107_design = F107_central + dF107_p95

Source noaa_swpc — NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center — solar and geomagnetic indices NOAA, retrieved 2026-09-01

The centre comes from sw_central_expectation, the spread from sw_uncertainty_growth, and the confidence is the 95th percentile that sw_uncertainty_growth publishes — so this is a value the mission should not exceed in 95 per cent of histories, not a value it will see. It used to be the subsystem's headline: l3_solar_ach_01, l3_solar_ach_02 and l3_solar_interface all restated it. §20 step 5 re-pointed the two closures at sw_f107_design_long and sw_f107_design_short, and §20 step 4 gave the interface the whole driver set, so this row now has no consumers at all. It is not wrong and it was not replaced — it answers a DIFFERENT question from the design rows. This is a one-sided 95th-percentile PERSISTENCE growth: how far might the flux drift from today's value over the mission. sw_f107_design_long is a band width: how wrong has the pattern historically been about the level a rotation sits at. Both are defensible and they are not the same question, which sw_f107_design_long's own question note says in as many words. A published number nothing reads is a number nobody checks, which is the standard this subsystem holds itself to elsewhere. THE ROW IS THEREFORE DEPRECATED, decided 2026-09-16. It stays readable with its whole history, the gate stops treating it as live, and the contract check refuses it as a NEW dependency — so nothing can pick it up again without that being a deliberate act. What it answers is still a real question, and if a design wants the persistence reading back it should be revived on purpose rather than found by accident.

fails when the record's own 95th percentile of daily F10.7 is 201 sfu, while this construction returns 228 at a five-year lead. The two answer different questions — the highest flux a day is likely to show, against how far the flux can move from its central expectation — and the second is the larger because it compounds where the centre sits with how wrong the centre can be. A reader who wants 'the 95th percentile of F10.7' wants the record, not this row.

Two of the numbers this subsystem exists to publish are a flux and an Ap to size to. A design value is not an expectation: an expectation is exceeded half the time, and a structure sized to be adequate half the time is not a design. So the value has two parts — where the flux is expected to be, and how far it can move from there over the time the mission is exposed — and the safety is entirely in the second part. The relation is an addition, and everything worth reviewing about it is in the two rows it adds and in what 'the 95th percentile' is the percentile OF.

  1. Start from why a centre is not enough. sw_central_expectation returns 86.85 sfu, which is a median-like quantity: the flux will be above it about half the time over the mission. Sizing a structure, a power budget or a propellant load to a figure that is exceeded half the time is not a margin, it is a coin toss.
  2. The quantity that has to be bounded is therefore not the flux but the DEPARTURE of the flux from its central expectation over the exposure time. That departure grows with the exposure: a mission of one month can be surprised less than a mission of five years.dF107(L) = F107(t + L) - F107_central(L)
  3. Bound the departure by a percentile of its measured distribution rather than by a multiple of a standard deviation. F10.7's distribution is not symmetric — it is bounded below near 64 sfu and has a long upper tail to 343 — so a mean-plus-k-sigma interval would be wrong on both sides. sw_uncertainty_growth measures the percentile directly from the record.F107_design(L) = F107_central(L) + dF107_p95(L)
  4. At the declared five-year mission that is 86.85 plus 113.29, and the split is the thing to notice: more than half the design value is margin. That is not excess conservatism, it is what a five-year exposure to a cyclical driver costs.F107_design = 86.85 + 113.29 = 200.14 sfu
  5. One consequence is worth stating before a reader misreads the number. Adding a percentile of the change to a central value compounds two uncertainties — where the centre is, and how far from it the flux can get — so the answer exceeds the record's own 95th percentile of daily flux, 201 sfu, without contradicting it. The two are answers to different questions.

Guards

  • F107_design ≥ 60 — below 60 sfu has never been observed and every relation reading F10.7 has no support there — env_f107's own floor, and a design value below it means the spread has been subtracted rather than added
  • F107_design ≤ 400 — above 400 sfu the exospheric temperature relation is extrapolated past the largest recorded daily value, which is env_f107's stated reason for the same bound. This guard is reachable: a central expectation near the top of its range plus a fifteen-year spread would exceed it, and it should refuse rather than hand a consumer a flux it cannot model

The reason travels with the guard. A guard whose reason is not written down gets deleted by the next person who finds it awkward.
