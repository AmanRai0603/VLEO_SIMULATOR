How much does the air itself heat the spacecraft?

Q = 0.5*rho*V^3*A*C_h sourced · larson_wertz

Source larson_wertz — Wertz, J. R., Everett, D. F. and Puschell, J. J. — Space Mission Engineering: The New SMAD Microcosm Press, 2011

Usually neglected, and at 200 km it should not be. It is a real term in the balance of a small, fast, low-flying vehicle.

3Where the simple version breaks

It takes a fixed heat transfer coefficient of 0.9, the share of the incoming air's energy that becomes heat, and applies it to the frontal area, with no stated assumptions or derivation. Above 100 kilowatts the vehicle is re-entering rather than orbiting.

No assumption stated. A multi-step relation always has at least one.

Outside 0 … 100000 W the row refuses rather than answers: heating cannot be negative; above 100 kW the vehicle is re-entering.

4Common wrong idea

That heating by the air can be ignored in orbit. At 200 kilometres it is a real term in the heat balance of a small, fast, low-flying vehicle.
