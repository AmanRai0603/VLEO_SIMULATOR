1Say it simply

The twisting force, or torque, that the difference in gravity's pull across the spacecraft applies to it, worked out with the spacecraft held 10 degrees off the local vertical. It depends on the size of the orbit and on how unevenly the spacecraft's mass is spread, and the attitude wheels must hold against it.

In plain words by an assistant, from what this sheet states — for its owner (gnc) to confirm.

2Now the real thing

How much torque does the gravity gradient apply?

T = (3*mu/(2*r^3))*|Iz - Iy|*sin(2*theta) sourced · larson_wertz

Source larson_wertz — Wertz, J. R., Everett, D. F. and Puschell, J. J. — Space Mission Engineering: The New SMAD Microcosm Press, 2011

3Where the simple version breaks

It is taken at one attitude, 10 degrees off the local vertical; a spacecraft held at another angle feels a different torque. Above 1 newton metre the number no longer describes a design that works, because no wheel that fits this vehicle can hold attitude against it.

No assumption stated. A multi-step relation always has at least one.

Outside 0 … 1 N.m the row refuses rather than answers: a torque magnitude cannot be negative; above 1 N.m no wheel that fits this vehicle can hold attitude.
