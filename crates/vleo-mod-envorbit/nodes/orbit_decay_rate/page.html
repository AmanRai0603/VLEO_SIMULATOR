explanation

1Say it simply

How fast the orbit shrinks if nothing makes up for the drag, in metres per second of orbit size lost. It depends on the air density, the vehicle's ballistic coefficient (how much it resists being slowed by the air) and the size of the orbit, and it feeds the service lifetime.

In plain words by an assistant, from what this sheet states — for its owner (systems) to confirm.

2Now the real thing

How fast does the orbit fall if nothing compensates the drag?

da/dt = -rho*sqrt(mu*a)/BC sourced · vallado2013

Source vallado2013 — Vallado, D. A. — Fundamentals of Astrodynamics and Applications, 4th edition Microcosm Press, 2013

3Where the simple version breaks

It uses the long-term average decay for a circular orbit. A decay faster than 10 metres per second is re-entry, not an orbit.

No assumption stated. A multi-step relation always has at least one.

Outside -10 … 0 m/s the row refuses rather than answers: a decay faster than 10 m/s of semi-major axis per second is re-entry, not an orbit; drag cannot raise an orbit; a positive value here is a sign error upstream.

4Common wrong idea

That drag could ever raise the orbit. It cannot; a positive decay rate here is a sign error upstream.
