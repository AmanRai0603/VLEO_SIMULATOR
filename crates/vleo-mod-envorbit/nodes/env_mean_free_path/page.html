How far a gas molecule travels, on average, between collisions at the flight altitude. It decides whether the air meets a spacecraft-sized body as separate molecules or as a continuous flow.

2Now the real thing

How far does a molecule travel between collisions?

lambda = 1/(sqrt(2)*n*sigma) sourced · us_std_1976

Source us_std_1976 — U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976 NOAA-S/T 76-1562

3Where the simple version breaks

It treats every molecule as a hard ball of one fixed effective size, whatever the gas. Below a millimetre a spacecraft-sized body would be in continuous flow; beyond a billion metres the idea has no operational meaning here.

No assumption stated. A multi-step relation always has at least one.

Outside 0.001 … 1000000000 m the row refuses rather than answers: a mean free path below a millimetre would put a spacecraft-scale body in continuum flow; beyond 1e9 m the concept of a mean free path has no operational meaning here.
