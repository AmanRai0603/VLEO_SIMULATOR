1Say it simply

The height over which the air's density falls by a fixed factor, the mathematical constant e. It is set by the local temperature, the average weight of the gas and gravity, and it says how quickly the air thins with height.

In plain words by an assistant, from what this sheet states — for its owner (environment) to confirm.

2Now the real thing

Over what altitude change does the density fall by a factor of e?

H = R*T/(M*g) sourced · vallado2013

Source vallado2013 — Vallado, D. A. — Fundamentals of Astrodynamics and Applications, 4th edition Microcosm Press, 2013

3Where the simple version breaks

It is a local figure at one altitude. The sheet holds it between 5 and 200 kilometres: less than 5 does not occur above the model base, and above 200 the atmosphere is effectively at one temperature throughout and the idea stops being useful.

No assumption stated. A multi-step relation always has at least one.

Outside 5 … 200 km the row refuses rather than answers: a scale height below 5 km does not occur above the model base; above 200 km the atmosphere is effectively isothermal and the concept stops being useful.
