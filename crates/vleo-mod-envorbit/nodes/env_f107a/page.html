It restates what the system layer was told; it does not decide it.

The 81-day mean stands in for the EUV averaged over three solar rotations, which is the timescale the whole thermosphere equilibrates on. Producing it for a window that has not happened yet is a forecast, and the subsystem that makes forecasts makes it. This row carries it inward.

It is a separate row from env_f107 rather than a derivation of it because the two are independently published members of the driver set: the study's hotday scenario carries hotmean's 81-day mean beneath it, so the pair is a lookup and not a relation.

Reading the answer

It is a MEAN over the window and the daily value beside it is a level over the same window, so at the hotmean scenario the two are equal and the thermosphere relation's departure term contributes nothing. That is a property of this scenario, not of the relation: on the hotday scenario the daily level is above its own mean and the departure term does work.

Whoever reads a departure of exactly zero as a defect should check which scenario is loaded before treating it as one.
