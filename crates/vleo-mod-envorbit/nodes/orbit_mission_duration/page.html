3Where the simple version breaks

It is one satellite's working life, not the service's. A constellation that replaces satellites sooner than a satellite's life has two mission lengths, and this is the shorter. It is only meaningful between six months and 15 years, the same range as the requirement it carries.

No assumption stated. A multi-step relation always has at least one.

Outside 0.5 … 15 yr the row refuses rather than answers: below six months the programme cannot amortise a satellite, so it is not the mission being designed; above 15 years the cost model, the degradation model and the battery cycle model are all extrapolated well past their fits.

4Common wrong idea

It is easy to read this as how long the service runs. It is one satellite's operating duration, and it restates the requirement rather than setting it.

explanation

It restates the requirement; it does not set it.

Mission length is a programme decision, so it is declared once, at layer 2, where the requirements are — `sys_mission_requirements_mission_duration`, five years, confirmed. This row is the subsystem-facing copy of that decision, and its relation is the identity because a copy that computes anything is a second opinion about how long the mission is.

Two rows declaring a mission length is two rows that can disagree, and the twelve rows reading this one would then be sized against whichever of the two their author happened to open. Carrying the number rather than re-declaring it is what makes that impossible.
