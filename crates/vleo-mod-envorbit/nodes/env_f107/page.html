The number the density chain is built on. It is no longer declared here: the solar-weather subsystem computes it over the mission window and publishes it through l3_solar_interface, layer 2 carries it as sys_space_environment_f10_7, and this row carries it into the subsystems that read it. It is a SUSTAINED level over the window, not the flux on a day. What the Sun is doing now is a different question and a different row — sw_f107_observed, which is what the subsystem's own forecast starts from.

3Where the simple version breaks

It is the hot sustained scenario's level, not the flux on any one day; a design that must survive its worst single day needs a different member of the set. It also made the modelled atmosphere cooler and thinner than the old figure of 150, so there is less drag but less margin against a real hot sky.

No assumption stated. A multi-step relation always has at least one.

Outside 60 … 400 the row refuses rather than answers: below 60 sfu has never been observed; the fit has no support there; above 400 sfu is beyond the largest recorded daily value, so the temperature relation is extrapolated.

4Common wrong idea

The drop from 150 to 104.07 was not the correction of an error: 150 was a defensible design default, and the new number is the solar record's own answer for this mission window.

It restates what the system layer was told; it does not decide it.

Which flux the design is sized to is a conclusion about a five-year window, and producing it is a subsystem's whole job — a record, a cycle analogue, a lead- weighted estimator and a spread, which is what the forty rows of solar weather are. A row here that declared its own number would be a second answer to that question, and the twelve rows reading this one would be sized against whichever of the two their author happened to open.

That is not hypothetical: it is what this row was until now. It declared 150 while the subsystem computed 104.07 over the same window, and the two were never compared because nothing read both.

It is the HOTMEAN scenario's sustained level, because that is the member l3_solar_interface publishes as its primary. The subsystem also publishes nominal, coldmean, hotday and coldday, and a design that must survive its worst single day rather than its worst sustained period wants a different member of that set than this row carries.

The value dropped from 150 to 104.07 when this wiring landed. That is not a correction of an error — 150 was a declared design default and is a defensible one — it is the record's own answer for this mission window replacing a round number, and it makes the modelled thermosphere COOLER and the density LOWER. A design sized on the new number has less drag and less margin against a real hot sky than one sized on the old, and §49 measures what moved.
