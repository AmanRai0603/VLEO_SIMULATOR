1Say it simply

How fast the spacecraft moves along its orbit, and so through the atmosphere, in metres per second. It is worked out from the distance to the Earth's centre, treating the orbit as a circle, and in this tool's band lies between 7000 and 8200 metres per second.

In plain words by an assistant, from what this sheet states — for its owner (systems) to confirm.

2Now the real thing

How fast is the spacecraft moving through the atmosphere?

V = sqrt(mu/r) sourced · vallado2013

Source vallado2013 — Vallado, D. A. — Fundamentals of Astrodynamics and Applications, 4th edition Microcosm Press, 2013

3Where the simple version breaks

It assumes a circular orbit with only the Earth pulling. At an eccentricity of 0.05, a measure of how far the orbit is from a circle, the speed varies by 5 per cent around the orbit, and a drag figure worked out at the mean speed underestimates the pass closest to the Earth.
