1Say it simply

How much noise the ground receiving system adds, expressed as a temperature in kelvin. It combines the antenna's own noise, the noise from losses in the line, and the receiver's own noise, taken at a 290 kelvin physical temperature and a receiver noise figure, the measure of noise it adds, of 1.2 decibels.

In plain words by an assistant, from what this sheet states — for its owner (comms) to confirm.

2Now the real thing

How much noise does the receiving system contribute?

T_s = T_a/L + T0*(L-1)/L + T0*(F-1) sourced · larson_wertz

Source larson_wertz — Wertz, J. R., Everett, D. F. and Puschell, J. J. — Space Mission Engineering: The New SMAD Microcosm Press, 2011

3Where the simple version breaks

It fixes the physical temperature at 290 kelvin and the noise figure at 1.2 decibels, and the sheet states no assumptions or derivation. Below 10 kelvin needs cryogenic cooling this ground segment lacks; above 3000 kelvin the receiving system is not usable.

No assumption stated. A multi-step relation always has at least one.

Outside 10 … 3000 K the row refuses rather than answers: below 10 K requires cryogenic cooling not present in this ground segment; above 3000 K the receiving system is not usable.
