1Say it simply

How many oxygen atoms strike each square metre of a forward-facing surface over the whole mission: the atomic oxygen density, times the flight speed, times the mission's length. It matters because the atoms wear material away; polyimide, for example, loses roughly 30 micrometres at a fluence of 1e22.

In plain words by an assistant, from what this sheet states — for its owner (mass) to confirm.

2Now the real thing

How many oxygen atoms strike a forward-facing surface over the mission?

F = n_O*V*t sourced · moe2005

Source moe2005 — Moe, K. and Moe, M. M. — Gas-surface interactions and satellite drag coefficients Planetary and Space Science 53, 2005

Polyimide erodes at about 3e-24 cm3 per incident atom, so 1e22 removes roughly 30 micrometres of it.

3Where the simple version breaks

It is for a surface facing forward into the flow, and it multiplies one density, one speed and one duration, so it is only as good as those single figures are for the whole mission. Above 1e26 atoms per square metre no known external material survives.

No assumption stated. A multi-step relation always has at least one.

Outside 0 … 100000000000000000000000000 the row refuses rather than answers: a fluence cannot be negative; above 1e26 atoms per square metre no known external material survives, so the design is not a design.
