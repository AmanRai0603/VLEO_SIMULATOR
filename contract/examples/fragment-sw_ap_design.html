2Now the real thing

What daily Ap is this design built to survive?

Ap_design(G) = ap at the top Kp of that G band: G1 -> 48, G2 -> 80, G3 -> 132 sourced · iaga_kp_ap

Source iaga_kp_ap — Bartels, J. — The standardized index Ks and the planetary index Kp, with the ap equivalent-amplitude table IATME Bulletin 12b, IUGG Publication Office, 1949; the 28-point table as published by IAGA and distributed by GFZ Potsdam

The design-side companion to sw_storm_return_level. That row says what the record will present over the mission; this says what the vehicle is built for, from the G level sw_storm_design_level declares. At the default G3 the answer is 132 and the record's five-year expectation is 158.4, and the gap between them is the finding.

3Where the simple version breaks

It is a bound, not a typical storm day: real G3 days in the record run from 15 to 96 with a median of 51. It knows only the size of a storm, not how long it lasts, so a one-day storm and the first day of a three-day storm look the same here, although a sustained storm heats the atmosphere far more.

Outside 40 … 140 the row refuses rather than answers: the lowest value this relation can return is 48, at G1. 4Common wrong idea

It is easy to read this and the once-per-mission storm level as competing answers. One is what the vehicle is built for and the other what the sky will present; the gap is closed by operations, not structure.

explanation

The G level is a name and the design needs a number, in the quantity the rest of the tree carries: daily planetary Ap. Getting from one to the other is not a conversion so much as a choice, because a G level constrains only the worst of a day's eight three-hourly slots and daily Ap is the mean of all eight. A single G3 day could have a daily Ap almost anywhere below the value it would reach if every slot sat at Kp 7. This row takes that upper value, and the whole of its content is the argument for taking the ceiling rather than the middle.

Each line below is derived from the one before, ending at the relation — which is sourced · iaga_kp_ap.

  1. A G level fixes the worst slot of the day and leaves the other seven unconstrained. Daily Ap is the mean of all eight, so a day labelled G3 can have almost any daily Ap up to the value it would reach if every slot were as bad as its worst.Ap_day = mean of eight three-hourly ap; G fixes only max Kp
  2. The record shows how wide that leaves it: days whose Kp_max is exactly 7 run from daily Ap 15 to 96, with a median of 51. There is no single Ap that 'is' G3.
  3. A design bound has to hold for the worst day inside the level it claims, so the ceiling is the right end of the band, not the middle. The ceiling is the daily Ap a day would reach if all eight slots sat at that level's Kp — which is just that Kp's tabulated ap, since the mean of eight equal values is the value.Ap_design(G) = ap(Kp of G) = 48, 80, 132 for G1, G2, G3
  4. That makes the row conservative by construction, and the size of the conservatism is measurable: the median G3 day is 51 and this row publishes 132, so no G3 day in 29 years came within a third of the design value. That is the price of a bound that cannot be violated by any day the level admits.
  5. It is a lookup on three integers and nothing between them is defined. The ap values at Kp 5, 6 and 7 are 48, 80 and 132 — ratios of 1.67 and 1.65, so the scale is close to geometric and a linear reading between levels would be wrong by tens of nanotesla. That is why the producer's range is bounded to integers and why this row does not interpolate.
  6. One thing the quantity cannot carry: daily Ap ignores everything about a storm except its size. A day at Ap 132 that recovers overnight and the first day of a three-day storm at the same Ap are the same number here, and the thermosphere does not treat them alike — density lags the driver, and a sustained storm heats it far more than one disturbed day. A drag design taking this number alone gets the peak and not the integral.

Reading the answer

The answer at G3 is 132 and the record's five-year expectation is 158.4 — the design value is BELOW what the mission should expect, and the gap is the finding this pair of rows exists to surface. It is also conservative within its own level in the opposite direction: no G3 day in 29 years came within a third of 132, whose typical value is 51. So the number is a bound on its level and not a description of it, and the two facts do not cancel — one is about the level chosen, the other about the sky.

Written by Aman Rai / 2026-10-03.

# Transcribed from crates/vleo-mod-solar/nodes/sw_ap_design/model.rs (HOLE 1 and its guards) — the method the code already runs.
# The published ap equivalent amplitude at the top Kp of each G band (iaga_kp_ap): G1 is Kp 5 is ap 48, G2 is Kp 6 is ap 80, G3 is Kp 7 is ap 132.
# A lookup on three integer levels, not an interpolation: the ap scale is close to geometric.
let out = 132
if g_level < 1.5 then
  set out = 48
else if g_level < 2.5 then
  set out = 80
end
if out < 40 then
  refuse "Designing to less than a minor storm (G1, Ap 48) is not a design case."
end
if out > 140 then
  refuse "Above G3's Ap 132 means a G level outside the range or a misread table."
end
return out

Guards

  • Ap_design ≥ 40 — the lowest value this relation can return is 48, at G1. A bound at 40 sits just under it and refuses anything that would design to less than a minor storm, which is not a design case: the record has 1358 days at G1 or above, 47 a year
  • Ap_design ≤ 140 — the highest this relation can return is 132, at G3, which is the top of the declared G range. A bound at 140 sits just above it and catches a G level outside the range or a misread table. It does NOT bound the sky: the record's largest daily Ap is 273 and the five-year return level is 158.4, both above this bound, and neither passes through this row

The reason travels with the guard. A guard whose reason is not written down gets deleted by the next person who finds it awkward.
