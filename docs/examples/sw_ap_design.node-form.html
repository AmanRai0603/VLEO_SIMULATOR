Answer first. This file asks for one node of the design to change — or for a new one — and it is the whole of the request: it needs no connection and nothing installed. Fill in what you know, say why it is changing, save a copy and send it to the developers. They check it, apply it, and the next release of the tool carries it, where you run it on your own inputs.

  1. What is asked. Every question the node answers, in the order it is read: what it is called and asks, said simply, the relation and where it comes from, the answer and its bounds, the derivation, and where the simple version breaks. Each question says why it is asked. Leave what you cannot answer as it is.
  2. Why it is changing. A node changes because a belief broke. If your changes move what the node computes — an input, the output, the model, the maths, the algorithm, how it is drawn — the section Why it is changing must say what was believed, what was tested, what we now know and what changes. Without it the developers apply only your wording. A worked example is in that section.
  3. What is not yours to change here. Where the node sits in the tree, its kind and its owner are the developers'. If an assistant helped with the relation itself — the equation, its steps or its derivation — say so: those are then derived by a developer, never taken from the form. Known values go under known values, with their source; they are recorded by a person.
